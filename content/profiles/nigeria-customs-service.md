  <p dir="auto" data-start="426" data-end="865"
  class="PDq2pG_selectionAnchorContainer">The <strong data-start="430"
  data-end="463">Nigeria Customs Service (NCS)</strong> is Nigeria’s principal
  customs administration, trade facilitation, revenue collection, and border
  management institution. The Service is responsible for administering customs
  laws, regulating the movement of goods across Nigeria’s international borders,
  collecting customs revenue, facilitating legitimate international trade, and
  preventing smuggling and other forms of illicit cross-border activity.<span
  aria-hidden="true" class="PDq2pG_selectionAnchor"></span></p>

  <p dir="auto" data-start="867" data-end="1112">With operations spanning
  <strong data-start="892" data-end="993">seaports, airports, land borders,
  inland terminals, and other designated points of entry and exit</strong>, the
  Nigeria Customs Service plays a central role in Nigeria’s trade, logistics,
  maritime, and supply-chain ecosystem.</p>

  <p dir="auto" data-start="1114" data-end="1496">At Nigeria’s <strong
  data-start="1127" data-end="1139">seaports</strong>, the NCS is a key
  government institution involved in the customs clearance and control of
  imported and exported cargo. Its activities directly influence the movement of
  goods through major Nigerian ports, including the <strong data-start="1359"
  data-end="1495">Lagos Port Complex, Tin Can Island Port, Lekki Deep Sea Port,
  Onne Port, Port Harcourt Port, Calabar Port, and other port
  facilities</strong>.</p>

  <p dir="auto" data-start="1498" data-end="1712">The Service combines <strong
  data-start="1519" data-end="1634">customs administration, cargo control,
  revenue collection, enforcement, risk management, and trade
  facilitation</strong> to protect Nigeria’s economic interests while supporting
  legitimate commerce.</p>

  <p dir="auto" data-start="1714" data-end="2038">A major part of the Nigeria
  Customs Service mandate is the facilitation of legitimate international trade.
  Through customs procedures, cargo documentation, valuation, classification,
  inspection, risk assessment, and clearance processes, the Service contributes
  to the efficiency of Nigeria’s import and export supply chains.</p>

  <p dir="auto" data-start="2040" data-end="2389">The NCS also has a significant
  <strong data-start="2071" data-end="2113">anti-smuggling and enforcement
  mandate</strong>. It works to detect and prevent the illegal movement of
  prohibited, restricted, undervalued, or undeclared goods across Nigeria’s
  borders. These activities contribute to economic security, protection of
  domestic industries, and the enforcement of Nigeria’s trade regulations.</p>

  <p dir="auto" data-start="2391" data-end="2699">The Nigeria Customs Service is
  therefore an important institution within Nigeria’s broader <strong
  data-start="2482" data-end="2529">maritime economy and Blue Economy
  ecosystem</strong>. Its activities intersect with ports, shipping, freight
  forwarding, logistics, international trade, cargo transportation, supply-chain
  management, and maritime security.</p>

  <p dir="auto" data-start="2701" data-end="2997">As Nigeria seeks to strengthen
  its position as a <strong data-start="2750" data-end="2824">regional maritime,
  logistics, and trade hub in West and Central Africa</strong>, the
  effectiveness of customs administration remains important to port efficiency,
  cargo movement, trade competitiveness, and the development of Nigeria’s
  maritime economy.</p>
