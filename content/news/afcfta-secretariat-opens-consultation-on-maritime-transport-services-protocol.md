<p>The AfCFTA Secretariat has opened a formal consultation period on the maritime transport component of the agreement's Trade in Services Protocol, inviting written submissions from carriers, port operators, national regulators, and industry associations across the continent.</p><p>The consultation covers two areas that have proved persistently difficult in earlier negotiating rounds: the harmonisation of national cabotage regimes, and the scope of liberalisation to be applied to port and auxiliary maritime services.</p><h3>Cabotage Is the Harder Half</h3><p>Cabotage reservation — restricting domestic coastal trade to nationally flagged, owned, and crewed vessels — sits at the centre of several member states' maritime industrial policy, Nigeria's most prominently. A continental services protocol that treats African-flagged tonnage as domestic for cabotage purposes would represent a substantial expansion of the addressable market for regional operators.</p><p>It would equally dilute the protection that national indigenous shipping programmes were built to provide, and the Secretariat's consultation notice acknowledges that member states have taken sharply divergent positions on where that balance should fall.</p><p><img src="https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1520939817895-060bdaf4fe1b?w=1200&amp;q=80&amp;fit=crop" alt="Coastal vessel operating in West African waters"></p><h3>Port Services Liberalisation</h3><p>The second strand covers terminal handling, pilotage, towage, bunkering, ship agency, and marine surveying. Several of these are already effectively open to foreign participation at major African gateways through concession arrangements; others remain reserved to state entities or nationally licensed providers.</p><p>Industry submissions close at the end of October, with a negotiating round scheduled to follow. The Secretariat has indicated that a consolidated draft text will be circulated to member states before the end of the year.</p>
