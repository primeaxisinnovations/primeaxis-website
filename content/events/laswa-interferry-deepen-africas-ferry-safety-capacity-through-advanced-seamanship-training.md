  As African governments intensify efforts to improve the safety, reliability
  and professionalism of inland water transport, investment in people is
  emerging as one of the sector's most strategic priorities. It is against this
  backdrop that the Lagos State Waterways Authority (LASWA), in partnership with
  Interferry, has successfully completed the second module of the Ferry Safety
  Development Programme (FSDP), further strengthening Africa's first structured
  professional development pathway for ferry operators.


  The completion of the Seamanship module represents more than another training
  milestone. It reflects a deliberate shift towards competency-based capacity
  development, recognising that sustainable improvements in ferry safety depend
  not only on vessels and infrastructure, but also on the knowledge, judgement
  and operational discipline of the people responsible for daily ferry
  operations.


  Delivered throughout June, the module focused on the practical foundations of
  professional seamanship, including ropes and knots, mooring and anchoring, and
  deck maintenance. Participants progressed through a blended learning model
  combining self-paced study, virtual mentoring, expert engagement and practical
  field exercises, ensuring that theoretical knowledge was reinforced through
  operational application.


  A key feature of the module was the "Ask the Expert" session held on 17 June,
  where participants interacted with Mr. Jeff Joyce, Interferry Mentor and Vice
  President, Marine Operations, Northumberland Ferries Limited. Drawing from
  decades of international operational experience, Joyce explored the realities
  of modern ferry operations, leadership at sea, safety management and
  seamanship standards, while encouraging participants to relate global best
  practices to the operational realities of African waterways.


  The programme's practical training was conducted on 24 June at Sea School,
  Apapa, under the coordination of Engr. Ukeme Sunday and facilitated by Sea
  School Coordinator, Dr. Ekundayo, with the support of seven experienced
  maritime instructors.


  The 50 participants were divided into three practical groups, receiving
  intensive instruction in rope identification, rope handling, knot tying, line
  management and the operational use of essential knots in ferry services.
  Beyond technical proficiency, the exercises strengthened teamwork, situational
  awareness, communication and safe onboard decision-making—competencies that
  remain fundamental to accident prevention and efficient vessel operations.


  Speaking on the significance of the programme, the Special Adviser to the
  Governor on Blue Economy, Mr. Oluwadamilola Emmanuel, emphasised that the
  practical component complements the programme's virtual mentoring and
  self-paced learning by exposing participants to real operational environments
  where internationally recognised practices can be translated into everyday
  operational competence.


  The programme also demonstrates the growing value of collaboration across the
  maritime ecosystem. LASWA acknowledged Lagos Ferry Services Company Limited
  (LAGFERRY) and Thesaurus Bay Limited for providing ferries for the practical
  exercises; Interferry for sponsoring the programme; Mrs. Shari Corrigan for
  her continued technical support; Mr. Jeff Joyce for sharing international
  expertise; and Dr. Ekundayo and the management of Sea School, Apapa, for
  facilitating the practical sessions. The Authority further recognised the
  Lagos State Government for sustaining investments in maritime safety, human
  capital development and the state's expanding blue economy agenda.


  What distinguishes the Ferry Safety Development Programme is its long-term
  approach to workforce development. Rather than focusing on isolated training
  interventions, the 18-month programme is structured to progressively build
  technical competence, operational leadership and a culture of continuous
  learning. This aligns with a growing global recognition that resilient ferry
  systems are built not only through regulatory oversight and modern
  infrastructure, but through competent professionals capable of applying
  international standards consistently in day-to-day operations.


  For Africa, where inland waterways continue to expand as vital transport
  corridors connecting communities, supporting commerce and reducing pressure on
  road networks, strengthening human capital is increasingly becoming a
  strategic investment in transport resilience, passenger safety and sustainable
  blue economy development. Programmes such as the FSDP therefore contribute not
  only to improving individual competencies but also to building institutional
  capacity capable of supporting safer ferry operations across the continent.


  With two modules successfully completed, the programme now advances to Module
  Three—Ferry Safety—in July 2026. Participants will undertake specialised
  learning in emergency preparedness, risk management and internationally
  recognised safety practices, continuing the programme's objective of
  developing a new generation of ferry professionals equipped to strengthen
  safety culture and operational excellence across Africa's inland waterways.
