  - question: >-
      The Nigerian Shippers' Council is evolving beyond its traditional role as
      the protector of shippers' interests into a broader economic regulator.
      What vision is driving this transformation?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">The maritime and logistics landscape has evolved

      significantly, and our regulatory framework must evolve with it. The
      Nigerian

      Shippers' Council, established in 1978, has played an important role in

      protecting the interests of shippers. However, today’s trade environment

      requires a stronger and more comprehensive regulatory institution.</p><p
      class="MsoNormal">Our vision is to build a modern regulator that promotes

      efficiency, transparency, fair competition, and sustainable growth across

      Nigeria’s port and logistics ecosystem. The proposed Nigerian Shipping and
      Port

      Economic Regulatory Agency (NPERA) Bill represents a key step in this

      direction. Once it receives Presidential Assent, it will strengthen the
      Council’s

      capacity to regulate economic activities within the port sector, promote
      fair

      competition, monitor compliance, protect stakeholders, and support
      national

      economic objectives under the Marine and Blue Economy
      agenda.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal"><o:p>&nbsp;</o:p>Ultimately,
      the goal is to create a regulatory environment

      that attracts investment, facilitates trade, and enhances Nigeria’s

      competitiveness in regional and global supply chains.</p>
  - question: >-
      One of your major priorities has been the development of Inland Dry Ports
      across Nigeria. Why are they so important to the country’s trade and
      logistics system?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">Inland Dry Ports are essential to building an
      efficient and

      integrated national logistics system. They enable cargo to be cleared
      closer to

      importers and exporters in the hinterland, reducing the need for
      businesses to

      travel to seaports for cargo processing. This improves efficiency across
      the

      entire supply chain.<o:p></o:p></p><p
      class="MsoNormal"><o:p>&nbsp;</o:p>Importers can receive goods closer to
      their businesses,

      while exporters can move products from Inland Dry Ports through the
      seaports to

      international markets.</p><p>








      </p><p class="MsoNormal">Beyond efficiency, Inland Dry Ports stimulate
      economic

      activity in inland regions, create jobs, reduce congestion at seaports,
      lower

      logistics costs, and support industrial development across the country. As

      Nigeria seeks to expand non-oil exports, Inland Dry Ports will play a
      critical

      role in connecting local producers to global markets.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      The proposed Nigerian Shipping and Port Economic Regulatory Agency (NPERA)
      Bill has generated significant interest within the maritime industry. What
      impact do you expect it to have once it becomes law?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">The existing Nigerian Shippers' Council Act was
      enacted

      decades ago, and while it has served its purpose, the industry has evolved

      significantly since then. The proposed legislation is designed to
      modernise the

      regulatory framework and align it with current realities in global trade
      and

      logistics.</p><p class="MsoNormal">Once enacted, it will strengthen the
      Council’s mandate by

      enhancing market oversight, promoting fair competition, improving
      regulatory

      compliance, and creating a more transparent and predictable operating

      environment within the port sector.</p><p class="MsoNormal">This is
      particularly important as Africa moves toward deeper

      economic integration under the African Continental Free Trade Area. AfCFTA

      represents a market of over 1.4 billion people with a combined GDP
      exceeding

      US$3 trillion, creating significant opportunities for trade expansion and

      investment across the continent.<o:p></o:p></p><p>










      </p><p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria must position itself to fully benefit
      from these

      opportunities through stronger institutions and efficient trade
      facilitation

      systems.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      The Council has consistently advocated for greater use of rail in cargo
      evacuation from the ports. Why is this so important?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">Rail remains one of the most efficient, safe, and

      cost-effective modes of transporting cargo over long distances. Currently,
      a

      large proportion of cargo from Nigerian seaports is moved by road. This
      places

      significant pressure on road infrastructure, increases congestion, raises

      logistics costs, and contributes to accidents and insecurity.</p><p
      class="MsoNormal">A modern logistics system must be multimodal,
      integrating

      seaports, rail, roads, and Inland Dry Ports into a seamless national
      network.</p><p class="MsoNormal">As the port economic regulator, our
      responsibility now

      extends beyond protecting shippers. It includes ensuring that cargo moves

      efficiently, safely, and competitively from ports to businesses,
      industries,

      and final consumers.<o:p></o:p></p><p>










      </p><p class="MsoNormal">Strengthening rail infrastructure and
      connectivity is

      therefore critical to reducing the cost of doing business and improving

      national competitiveness.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      As implementation of the African Continental Free Trade Area gathers
      momentum, what reforms should Nigeria prioritise to maximise its benefits?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">AfCFTA presents one of the most significant economic

      opportunities for Africa in decades. To fully benefit, African countries
      must

      continue harmonising trade laws, customs procedures, and regulatory
      frameworks.</p><p class="MsoNormal">There is also a need to strengthen
      intellectual property

      protection to encourage innovation and safeguard investments across the

      continent. Investment frameworks must be further improved to attract
      foreign

      direct investment and create a competitive environment for businesses
      operating

      across African markets.<o:p></o:p></p><p>






      </p><p class="MsoNormal">However, trade liberalisation alone is not
      enough. It must

      be supported by strong institutions, efficient processes, and predictable

      regulatory systems that make cross-border trade faster and more
      transparent.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      Nigeria continues to lose cargo to neighbouring ports. What reforms are
      needed to improve competitiveness?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">Port competitiveness depends on efficient
      infrastructure,

      effective regulation, and seamless logistics.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">First, continuous investment in modern port
      infrastructure

      is essential, including road and rail connectivity, cargo handling
      systems, and

      logistics technology. Second, a strong and predictable regulatory
      framework is

      required to ensure transparency, attract investment, and maintain fair

      competition. Third, digitalisation and cybersecurity are becoming
      increasingly

      important as global trade systems become more technology-driven.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Regional cooperation also plays a critical role.
      Under

      AfCFTA, African countries must collaborate more closely on trade
      facilitation,

      enforcement, and logistics integration.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Ultimately, Nigeria’s competitiveness will depend on
      how

      efficiently cargo moves through its ports and how predictable the overall

      business environment becomes.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      What reforms are required to strengthen the logistics chain from seaports
      to final destinations?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">An efficient logistics system requires clear legal
      and

      operational frameworks governing the entire transport chain. Whether cargo

      moves by road or rail, there must be a well-defined liability regime
      covering

      the movement of goods from seaports to Inland Dry Ports and final
      destinations.

      This ensures clarity for shipping companies, logistics providers, and
      cargo

      owners, while improving accountability across the value chain.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">There is also a need to harmonise existing legal
      frameworks

      governing the carriage of goods to remove uncertainty and improve
      efficiency.

      Stakeholder engagement remains central to this process, as sustainable
      reforms

      require collaboration between government, regulators, and private sector

      actors.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Ultimately, the objective of the Nigerian Shippers'
      Council

      is to strengthen trade efficiency, support economic growth, and improve

      Nigeria’s position within regional and global supply
      chains.<o:p></o:p></p>
