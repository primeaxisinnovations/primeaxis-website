      ANLCA has been part of Nigeria’s trade and customs landscape for 72 years.
      Looking beyond the anniversary celebrations, what do you consider the
      association’s most significant contributions to the freight-forwarding
      profession, Nigeria’s wider trade ecosystem and how has its role evolved
      over those seven decades?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal" style="text-align: left;">ANLCA’s journey has been
      one of immense impact on the

      shipping and trade industry. One cannot tell the story of freight
      forwarding

      and customs brokerage in Nigeria without acknowledging the contribution of
      the

      elders and senior colleagues who built the association over the years.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">ANLCA has been there for decades. Even with internal

      challenges and the emergence of breakaway groups, the association has
      shown

      what I would describe as nine lives. It has survived different periods and

      remained relevant to the industry.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">One of the major achievements of the current
      administration

      has been the recognition that peace and stability within the association
      are

      necessary for it to succeed. The intervention and support of the

      Comptroller-General of Customs Bashir Adewale Adeniyi also played an
      important

      role because the Nigeria Customs Service needs a stable and functional
      freight-forwarding

      community to achieve its revenue objectives.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">ANLCA has over 3,000 corporate members across
      Nigeria, and

      the contribution of these practitioners to Customs revenue cannot be
      ignored.

      Customs needs the freight-forwarding industry, and the freight-forwarding

      industry needs Customs. There has to be collaboration.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">ANLCA also plays an important role in promoting
      compliance.

      We tell our members and other practitioners that they must comply with
      Customs

      regulations, pay the right duties and ensure that cargo is properly
      declared.&nbsp;</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Our contribution goes beyond Customs
      revenue.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">We facilitate imports and exports. We ensure that
      goods come

      into the country and that Nigerian exports go out so that Nigeria can earn

      foreign exchange and grow its GDP. We facilitate the movement of cargo to

      manufacturers, merchants, warehouses and logistics
      operators.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">All of these activities create employment and support
      the

      wider economy.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">So, ANLCA’s contribution is not just about
      representing

      freight forwarders. We are part of the machinery that keeps trade
      moving.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      Freight forwarders operate where policy meets the practical reality of
      moving cargo. What are the biggest challenges confronting Nigerian customs
      agents and freight forwarders today, and which of these challenges do you
      believe are symptoms of deeper problems within the country’s trade
      facilitation system?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">The biggest challenge is policy changes coming within
      very

      short periods.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">When government introduces a policy, there should be
      a

      timeframe that allows people to understand it, amend their plans and
      determine

      what they can do within the new system. Policy should not come as a
      shock.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">There must be a concession or transition period where

      necessary. You cannot just introduce a policy and expect people to
      immediately

      adjust without giving them time to plan.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Take the issue of green taxation and electric
      vehicles, for

      example. You cannot tell people to go and buy electric vehicles when the

      necessary charging infrastructure is not available. There has to be a
      realistic

      timeframe.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">There are also situations where government announces
      a

      concession or zero-duty regime, but the process on the Customs platform
      does

      not immediately reflect the policy. You may still need documentation or
      approval

      from the Ministry of Finance before the shipment can be cleared.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">If government says something is zero-rated, then the
      system

      should be configured to reflect it. Otherwise, the policy has not really

      achieved its purpose.&nbsp;</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">We also need stronger recognition of ANLCA’s
      contribution.

      ANLCA should have a seat at the table when decisions affecting our
      industry are

      being made.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">And I ask: why can an ANLCA veteran who has spent
      years

      doing well as a Customs broker not have an opportunity to become part of
      the

      Nigeria Customs Service?</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">These are practitioners who understand the system
      from the

      operational side. Their experience can be valuable to
      government.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      Nigeria has invested heavily in port reforms, Customs modernisation and
      digitalisation. Yet importers and exporters continue to complain about
      delays, multiple processes, costs and unpredictability. From the
      perspective of someone operating inside the system, where have we
      genuinely made progress and where are we still solving the same problems
      we were confronting years ago?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal"><img
      src="https://n0jyvquhgs2welj7.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/uploads/1787109972083-003217f2-chatgpt-image-aug-19-2026-04-23-49-am.webp"
      alt=""></p><p class="MsoNormal">Things have improved, but there is still
      much more to be

      done.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal"><o:p>&nbsp;</o:p>When we travel to other countries
      and meet practitioners in

      the same industry, we see how their systems work. That comparison makes
      you

      realise that there are still areas where Nigeria needs to improve.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">One of the major complaints is demurrage. Demurrage
      is an

      additional cost, and ultimately the final consumer bears that cost.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Cargo clearance does not end simply because Customs
      has

      examined the cargo. The process is a chain, and the cargo is not truly
      cleared

      until it gets to the owner, user or warehouse.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">We now have different digital and clearance
      initiatives,

      including the Single Window efforts, FIRS processes and the B'Odogwu
      system of

      Customs. But then you can have a situation where you have cleared a cargo
      and

      you are at the point of releasing it, and another government agency
      intervenes.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Let me give you an example from the operational
      experience

      we are seeing.&nbsp;</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">You can clear your cargo without any issue, get to
      the

      releasing point and then the Maritime Police stops the cargo. You may then
      be

      asked to go back to the Nigerian Shippers’ Council for another
      clearance.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">You can lose an entire week going back and
      forth.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">And in many of these cases, no problem is eventually
      found

      and there is no clear justification for the delay. But that one week has

      already been lost.&nbsp;</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Who bears the cost?</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">The demurrage goes back to the importer and
      eventually the

      end user.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">That is why I keep saying that cargo clearance is a
      chain.

      Once one link in that chain is affected, the entire process is
      defeated.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">You may have Customs making efforts to fast-track
      cargo, but

      if that cargo gets stuck at another intervention point, what has been
      achieved?</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">We need to examine the operations of the Maritime
      Police at

      the port and the coordination between all the agencies involved in cargo

      movement.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">If a cargo has already been examined and cleared, we
      need to

      understand why another process should unnecessarily hold it
      up.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">The question is: what is the essence of improving one
      part

      of the system if another part can still create an avoidable
      delay?<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">That is where I believe we need much stronger
      coordination.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      Digitalisation is increasingly being presented as the answer to
      inefficiency in ports and trade. But does putting an inefficient process
      online actually make trade more efficient? What should Nigeria prioritise
      to ensure digital transformation genuinely reduces friction, improves
      transparency and makes the work of freight forwarders easier?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">What will make the system easier is for every
      government

      agency involved in cargo clearance to be properly onboarded onto the
      Single

      Window and to operate according to its mandate.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Every agency should raise its issues at the
      appropriate

      stage and within the appropriate timeframe.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">The problem is when you have completed your
      processes, you

      want to write your TDO, obtain your gate pass and move your cargo, and
      that is

      when an arm of government comes up with another intervention.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">That affects every effort that has been made earlier
      in the

      process.&nbsp;</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Digitalisation should not simply mean putting
      existing

      processes online. The agencies must be integrated and the system must be
      able

      to identify all requirements before the cargo reaches the final stage.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">If an agency has an issue with a shipment, that issue
      should

      come up early enough for the freight forwarder to address
      it.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">You should not wait until the cargo is ready to leave
      before

      stopping it.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">The whole essence of the Single Window is to make the

      process seamless, transparent and predictable.<o:p></o:p></p>

      <p class="MsoNormal">We have the resources. We have the technology. What

      <p class="MsoNormal">When you travel to other African countries, you see

      <p class="MsoNormal">Take Ghana, for example. You see transparency at the

      predictable.<o:p></o:p></p>

      <p class="MsoNormal">For Nigeria to take full advantage of AfCFTA, we must

      <p class="MsoNormal">If we fix transparency and make our processes

  - question: >-

      framework is actually improving competitiveness rather than simply

    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">Now that the NPERA Bill has been signed, I believe
      this is

      an opportunity for the Nigerian Shippers’ Council to demonstrate the
      strength

      of the new regulatory framework.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Before now, many stakeholders saw the Council as a
      toothless

      bulldog because it could not effectively sanction some of the shipping

      companies when they acted improperly.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">That is one of the things we need to change.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">What affects us most as freight forwarders is the
      behaviour

      and practices of shipping companies.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Take container deposits, for example. We do not need

      unnecessary container deposit practices. Shipping companies should also
      have

      proper holding bays for empty containers.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Our trailers and every available piece of land should
      not

      become dumping grounds for containers.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">The regulator should look at these
      practices.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">We also need to address terminal operations,
      overbilling and

      operational inefficiencies. There are situations where people are forced
      to pay

      through their noses simply to move their cargo.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">These are some of the reasons businesses consider
      taking

      their trade to other countries.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Turnaround time matters. Delays matter. Cost
      matters.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">If these issues are properly tackled, the environment
      will

      become more conducive for people to thrive and conduct maritime business
      in

      Nigeria.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">The new regulatory framework should give the Nigerian

      Shippers’ Council the authority to speak and, where necessary, sanction
      erring

      shipping companies.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">For me, the success of NPERA should be measured by
      whether

      the port becomes more competitive, more transparent and less costly for
      the

      people who actually use it.<o:p></o:p></p>
