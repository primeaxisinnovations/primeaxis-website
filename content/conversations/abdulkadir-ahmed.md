  <p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria’s maritime future will ultimately depend not only
  on

  the ships, ports and infrastructure it builds, but on the competence of the

  people who operate them.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">As the global shipping industry becomes increasingly
  shaped

  by stringent safety requirements, technological change, environmental
  standards

  and increasingly sophisticated marine operations, the quality of Nigeria’s

  maritime workforce will determine how effectively the country can participate

  in and compete within the global maritime economy.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria has made important investments in maritime

  infrastructure, regulation and training.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">Yet a fundamental challenge remains: are we developing
  the

  depth of competence and practical experience required to operate safely,

  efficiently and competitively at international standards?</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">The challenge goes beyond training alone. It encompasses
  the

  quality of maritime education, access to meaningful sea-time, practical

  exposure, mentorship, professional development, retention of skilled personnel

  and the ability of industry, regulators and training institutions to work

  together to build a sustainable talent pipeline.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">There are encouraging examples of industry led
  intervention.

  The collaboration between the Maritime Academy of Nigeria and NLNG Shipping
  and

  Marine Services has created opportunities for Nigerian cadets to gain
  practical

  onboard experience, while NSML’s broader investment in maritime training and

  human capacity development demonstrates the important role that industry can

  play in strengthening the country’s maritime workforce.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">But the bigger question is how these efforts can
  contribute

  to a stronger and more coordinated national capacity development framework one

  capable of producing professionals who are not only certified, but genuinely

  competent, safety conscious and prepared for the demands of modern
  shipping.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">In this Executive Conversation, Abdulkadir Ahmed,
  Managing

  Director and Chief Executive Officer of NLNG Shipping and Marine Services

  Limited, discusses the state of Nigeria’s maritime competencies, the role of

  industry in developing practical skills, the domestication of international

  shipping standards, safety culture, sea-time opportunities and the
  capabilities

  Nigeria needs to build a globally competitive maritime workforce for the next

  decade.<o:p></o:p></p>
