  - question: >-
      TTP began with a specific challenge around truck movement and port access
      in Nigeria, but the company’s ambitions today appear much broader. What
      problem did you originally set out to solve with TTP and Ètò, and at what
      point did you begin to see the opportunity to build something much larger,
      a digital infrastructure layer for freight and logistics?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">TTP was created to solve a very specific problem: the

      disorder around truck movement in and out of the Apapa and Tin Can Ports,
      with

      expansion into other ports across Nigeria. Before Ètò, truck access was
      largely

      driven by physical queues, multiple layers of human intervention, and
      limited

      visibility, creating congestion, delays, inefficiencies, and opportunities
      for abuse.&nbsp;</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Ètò introduced a digital mechanism for scheduling and

      controlling truck movement, but we quickly realised that the underlying
      problem

      was bigger than truck access. A truck does not operate in isolation; it is
      part

      of a chain connecting cargo owners, terminals, ports, transporters,
      regulators,

      roads and ultimately the final destination.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">That is what led to our broader vision. We began to
      see the

      opportunity to build the digital infrastructure that connects these moving

      parts. Today, our focus is intelligent mobility infrastructure using

      technology, data and physical infrastructure to make the movement of
      people,

      vehicles and cargo more predictable and efficient.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      Ètò has significantly changed the way truck access to Nigeria’s major
      ports is scheduled and managed, with millions of truck movements processed
      through the system. At the same time, congestion, ticket manipulation and
      other operational challenges continue to generate debate among
      stakeholders. From TTP’s perspective, what has Ètò genuinely solved, what
      challenges remain, and what have the past few years taught you about the
      limits of technology when the wider logistics ecosystem is not working
      efficiently?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">Ètò has fundamentally changed the way truck access is
      scheduled

      and managed. We have processed millions of truck movements through the

      platform, while helping reduce truck turnaround times from weeks to days
      and

      significantly improving visibility and predictability around port
      access.&nbsp;</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">But technology cannot solve every problem in a
      logistics

      ecosystem. Ètò can provide a controlled digital process; it cannot, on its
      own,

      eliminate poor road conditions, terminal delays, human interference,

      non-compliance, or inefficiencies elsewhere in the supply chain.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Some of the challenges we continue to see, including

      operational challenges, are reminders that digitisation must go beyond
      putting

      an existing process online. It must remove unnecessary human intervention,

      strengthen identity and access controls, create traceability and connect

      systems across stakeholders.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Our biggest lesson is that technology is an enabler,
      not a

      substitute for systemic reform. Sustainable efficiency requires
      technology,

      infrastructure, regulation, enforcement, and stakeholder collaboration to
      move

      together.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      TTP is now moving beyond basic truck call-up into areas such as electronic
      barriers, vehicle identification, traffic management, SaaS solutions,
      National Single Window integration and other digital tools. Are we
      witnessing the evolution of Ètò from a truck scheduling platform into a
      broader digital freight management ecosystem, and what could that
      ecosystem ultimately look like?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">Yes. Ètò was the starting point, not the
      destination.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">We are progressively building capabilities around
      electronic

      tagging (Etag), electronic access control, traffic management, smart
      parking,

      SaaS solutions, terminal visibility and integration with other digital
      systems

      across the logistics chain.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">The long-term opportunity is an intelligent freight
      ecosystem where the movement of a truck and its cargo can be planned,
      verified, monitored, and optimised across the entire journey, not just at
      the port gate.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Imagine a system where a vehicle’s digital identity
      via

      E-tag/RFID, booking, cargo documentation, terminal appointment, route,
      access

      permissions and arrival are digitally connected. That creates a single
      flow of

      information across stakeholders and enables better decisions using
      real-time

      data.&nbsp;</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">That is the direction we are pursuing: moving from
      truck

      scheduling to intelligent mobility infrastructure that supports the
      broader

      freight economy.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      A port can become more efficient internally and still lose competitiveness
      if cargo cannot move efficiently beyond the terminal gates. Where do you
      believe the biggest remaining inefficiencies lie along Nigeria’s
      port-to-destination corridors, and how should government, ports,
      terminals, transport operators, cargo owners and technology providers work
      differently to create a genuinely seamless freight chain?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">One of Nigeria’s biggest logistics challenges is that
      we

      often optimise individual parts of the chain rather than the journey as a

      whole.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">A terminal can improve its internal processes, but if
      trucks

      spend hours waiting outside, encounter poorly coordinated corridors or
      face

      delays at their next point of call, the overall supply chain remains

      inefficient.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">We need to think in terms of end-to-end cargo
      journeys.

      Government should provide the policy, infrastructure and regulatory
      environment;

      ports and terminals should optimise cargo handling and appointments;
      transport

      operators should embrace standards and technology; cargo owners should
      improve

      planning; and technology providers should connect the data and processes
      that

      sit between these stakeholders.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">The goal should not simply be a more efficient port.
      It

      should be a predictable freight corridor from origin to
      destination.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      Nigeria is one of Africa’s most important trade gateways, while AfCFTA is
      creating new possibilities for intra-African commerce. TTP has also spoken
      about taking its technology and logistics model beyond Nigeria. Could
      systems such as Ètò become part of the digital infrastructure supporting
      cross-border freight across West Africa, and what would need to change for
      countries, ports, customs authorities and logistics operators to build
      connected systems that can work together rather than operate largely as
      separate national platforms?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">Absolutely. Nigeria has the scale, market and
      strategic

      position to play a major role in building Africa’s digital logistics

      infrastructure.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">But cross-border logistics cannot work efficiently if
      every

      country operates completely independent digital systems that cannot
      communicate

      with one another. The future requires connected systems between ports,
      customs

      systems, border agencies, transport platforms, cargo owners and logistics

      operators.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Ètò provides an example of how technology can bring

      structure, visibility and accountability to a complex freight environment.
      The

      next opportunity is to apply those principles across borders.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">For West Africa in particular, we should be thinking
      about

      common digital standards for vehicle and cargo identification, electronic

      documentation, data exchange, payments and corridor management.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">AfCFTA will create opportunities for more
      intra-African

      trade, but trade agreements alone do not move cargo. We also need the
      digital

      and physical infrastructure that allows goods to move across borders

      predictably and efficiently.<o:p></o:p></p>
  - question: >-
      Truck congestion is not only a transport problem; it affects fuel
      consumption, cargo costs, emissions, productivity and ultimately the
      competitiveness of African trade. How much economic and environmental
      value could Nigeria and the wider region unlock by reducing truck waiting
      time, unnecessary journeys and inefficient corridor operations and where
      do you see the biggest opportunities for technology to translate logistics
      efficiency into lower trade costs?
    answer: >-
      <p class="MsoNormal">The economic cost of inefficiency is enormous. Every
      hour a

      truck spends waiting results in fuel consumption, lost driver time,

      underutilised vehicle capacity, and additional costs passed through the
      supply

      chain.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">The environmental impact follows the same pattern.
      Reducing

      unnecessary idling, empty trips, and repeated journeys reduces fuel
      consumption

      and emissions.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">This is where technology can make a measurable
      difference.

      Better scheduling reduces waiting.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Vehicle and cargo visibility improves
      planning.&nbsp;</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">Digital access control reduces unnecessary movements
      and

      human intervention. Traffic management systems can optimise the use of

      available road and terminal capacity.</p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">We should therefore stop looking at logistics
      technology

      simply as an administrative tool. It is an economic and environmental

      efficiency tool.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">If we can make freight movement more predictable, we
      can

      lower logistics costs, improve asset utilisation, reduce emissions and
      make

      Nigerian trade more competitive.<o:p></o:p></p>

      <p class="MsoNormal">Truck transit parks will also play an important role

      vehicles reach constrained areas.<o:p></o:p></p>


      <p class="MsoNormal">For Nigeria to get there, we need to start now by

      in connected digital infrastructure, reliable physical infrastructure,
      common

      <p class="MsoNormal">Our ambition should be bigger than making Nigerian
      ports

  <p class="MsoNormal">Africa’s trade ambitions increasingly depend not only on
  physical

  ports, roads and border infrastructure, but on the digital systems that
  connect

  cargo, vehicles, terminals, regulators and trade corridors.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">As Nigeria seeks to strengthen its position as a gateway
  to

  African commerce under the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA), the

  next frontier is moving beyond isolated logistics processes towards connected,

  predictable and data-driven freight systems.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">Truck Transit Parks Limited (TTP), operator of the Ètò

  electronic truck call-up system, began by addressing the disorder surrounding

  truck movement into and out of Nigeria’s major ports.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">Its ambitions have since expanded towards what the
  company

  describes as intelligent mobility infrastructure, bringing together
  technology,

  data, connectivity and physical infrastructure to improve the movement of

  people, vehicles and cargo.</p>


  <p class="MsoNormal">In this PrimeAxis Executive Conversation, Jama
  Onwubuariri,

  Managing Director of Truck Transit Parks Limited, discusses the evolution of

  Ètò, the limits of technology without wider systemic reform, the emerging

  digital freight ecosystem, the gaps along Nigeria’s port-to-destination

  corridors, the potential for connected logistics systems across West Africa,

  and what Nigeria must do to become a more predictable and competitive gateway
  for

  African trade.<o:p></o:p></p>
