<p class="MsoNormal">Eight years after acquisition, a national maritime asset has
moved through different locations, deployment plans and PPP arrangements.
PrimeAxis examines what happened, what remains to be established and what
Nigeria must do next.&nbsp;</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">There are infrastructure projects that fail because they
were never built.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">There are others that fail because nobody needs them.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And then there are projects that present a more difficult
question: the asset exists, the need exists, the economic case exists&nbsp; but the system required to make the asset
work has taken years to assemble.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria’s modular floating dock belongs in that third
category</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Acquired by the Nigerian Maritime Administration and Safety
Agency (NIMASA) in 2018 at a reported cost of about ₦50 billion, the floating
dock was intended to become a major piece of Nigeria’s ship-repair
infrastructure.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Its intended benefits were straightforward.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Bring more vessel repairs home.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Reduce dependence on foreign dry-docking facilities.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Retain foreign exchange.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Create jobs.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Develop technical skills.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Support indigenous shipping.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Generate revenue.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Strengthen Nigeria’s maritime industrial base.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Eight years later, the question is no longer whether the
floating dock has potential.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The question is:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Why has Nigeria taken so long to convert that potential into
economic value?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And as the government continues the deployment process,
PrimeAxis believes Nigerians deserve more than another assurance that the dock
is “coming soon.”</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">They deserve to know where the asset stands&nbsp; physically, technically, financially and
commercially.<o:p></o:p></p>

<h3>THIS IS NOT A STORY ABOUT A DOCK ALONE</h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">It would be easy to reduce the floating dock story to an
expensive government asset that has spent years awaiting commercial deployment.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That would miss the larger issue.&nbsp;</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The floating dock is a test of Nigeria’s ability to turn
maritime infrastructure investment into maritime economic activity.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Because the dock was never intended to exist simply as a
floating piece of steel.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It was intended to operate as part of an ecosystem.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">A location.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">A berth.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Anchoring infrastructure.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Workshops.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Marine engineers.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Ship-repair specialists.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Classification and certification.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Spare parts.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Insurance.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Customers.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">A commercial operator.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">A viable PPP.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And, ultimately, vessels coming in for repairs and leaving
the facility ready to return to service.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Without those elements, the ₦50 billion asset cannot deliver
the full economic purpose for which it was acquired.</p>

<h3>2018: THE PROMISE<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA took delivery of the floating dock in 2018. The
facility was presented as a strategic investment in Nigeria’s ship-repair
capacity, with expected benefits including employment, wealth creation and
revenue generation.&nbsp;</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The logic was compelling.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria is a maritime nation with a substantial coastal,
offshore and cabotage fleet.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Yet significant vessel repairs have historically been
carried out outside the country.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That means repair expenditure can leave Nigeria with the
vessel.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Specialist services can leave.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Technical exposure can leave.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And opportunities to develop a domestic maritime industrial
supply chain can leave with them.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The floating dock was intended to capture part of that
economic activity domestically.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">But the operational arrangements surrounding the asset took
several years to develop.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That history is central to understanding the project.</p><h3>THE FIRST QUESTION: WHY WAS THE OPERATIONAL SYSTEM NOT IN
PLACE?</h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">By 2021, the dock had still not entered commercial service.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA was working through the approvals and PPP
arrangements required for its operation.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">In September 2021, the Infrastructure Concession Regulatory
Commission (ICRC) issued a Certificate of Compliance for the Outline Business
Case for operation of the Modular Floating Dockyard. The proposed management
arrangement was structured as a Public-Private Partnership under which the
facility would be operated, maintained and transferred. ICRC also noted that
issues including security, accessibility and complementary infrastructure had
been considered in the business case.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That raises important infrastructure questions:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Where exactly would the dock operate?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Who would operate it?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What would the commercial model be?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What site preparation would be required?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What dredging, mooring and anchoring infrastructure would be
necessary?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What approvals would be required?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What would happen to the asset while those arrangements were
being completed?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">These are not peripheral issues.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">They determine whether an infrastructure asset can operate
as a commercial facility.</p>

<h3>THE PPP: ANOTHER PIECE OF THE PUZZLE<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">The government subsequently moved toward a concession
arrangement.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">In December 2022, ICRC reported that the Federal Executive
Council had approved the Management Contract to Operate, Maintain and Transfer
the NIMASA Floating Dry Dock. ICRC's project record identifies J. Marine
Logistics as the preferred bidder and records FEC approval on December 21,
2022.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The arrangement was expected to provide a commercial
structure for operation and management of the facility.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Contemporary reporting put projected revenue to NIMASA at
about $65.6 million, approximately ₦27.2 billion at the time, over the 15 year
period, alongside projected employment benefits.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Those were projections.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And projections are useful.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">But projections are not performance.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The relevant question is:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What is the current status of the concession and its
commercial implementation?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That distinction matters.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Government can approve.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Agencies can announce.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Private partners can be identified.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Sites can be handed over.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">But economic value appears when the facility actually
operates and generates measurable activity.</p>

<h3>2023: THE “FINAL LAP”<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">In August 2023, NIMASA announced that it had taken over
areas leased to it by the Nigerian Ports Authority at Continental Shipyard for
the operation of the Modular Floating Dock.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The leased areas included the dolphin jetty, waterfront
areas, an administrative block and construction, welding, mechanical and civil
maintenance workshops.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA described the takeover as the “final lap” in the
deployment process.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA subsequently handed the site to Melsmore as technical
partners.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">According to NIMASA, Melsmore was expected to work with the
Nigerian Ports Authority on site management and risk assessment in preparation
for moving the floating dock to Continental Shipyard for full operations.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA also stated that Damen Shipyard, the original
manufacturer, would be invited to participate in recommissioning the dock.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The agency requested detailed deliverables, key performance
indicators and timelines from the technical partners.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">By this stage, the project had a designated operational
site, a technical partner and an established PPP framework.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The next requirement was execution.</p>

<h3>2024: “SLOWLY BUT STEADILY”<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">In February 2024, NIMASA announced that it had moved the
floating dock to a jetty at Standard Flour Mills in Apapa.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The agency explained that the move was preparatory.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Heavy cylindrical spuds, each weighing about 50 tonnes, were
being prepared for installation before the dock would be moved to its scheduled
operational base at Continental Shipyard.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The then Director-General, Bashir Jamoh, described the
process as moving “slowly but steadily” towards its destination. NIMASA said
the spuds had to be firmly installed before the dock could be towed to
Continental Shipyard.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The development illustrates an important technical point.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Deployment of a floating dock is not simply a matter of
towing the facility to a berth.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The berth has to be suitable.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The seabed has to be suitable.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The anchoring system has to be installed.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The surrounding infrastructure has to be ready.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And the technical and commercial arrangements have to support
operations.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA's own documentation shows that these elements formed
part of the deployment process.</p>

<h3>SO WHERE IS THE DOCK?<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">This is where PrimeAxis believes greater transparency is
necessary.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The public record contains references to several locations
over the years, including the Nigerian Naval Dockyard, Standard Flour Mills in
Apapa and Continental Shipyard, which NIMASA has identified as the scheduled
operational base.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA officially stated in February 2024 that the floating
dock had been moved to Standard Flour Mills in Apapa in preparation for its
movement to Continental Shipyard.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Earlier NIMASA documentation records the Naval Dockyard as
the dock's location before the planned relocation to the NPA's Continental
Shipyard site.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That makes the following questions reasonable and
answerable:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What is the floating dock’s exact current physical location?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Not its intended location.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Not its operational base.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Not the site leased for it.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Its physical location.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And alongside that:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><ul><li>Who currently has custody of the asset?</li><li>Who is responsible for its security?</li><li>What is its current physical condition?</li><li>Has it been moved from its previous location?</li><li>When was it last physically inspected?</li><li>What technical work has been completed?</li><li>What work remains outstanding?</li></ul><o:p></o:p></p>













<p class="MsoNormal">For a national maritime asset valued at about ₦50 billion in
public reporting, these are basic status questions.</p>

<h3>THE MONEY QUESTION<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">This may be the most sensitive part of the investigation.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Over the years, different media reports have published
different estimates of the cost associated with keeping the dock out of
commercial operation.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">One widely cited figure was approximately $30,000 per day in
demurrage or holding-related costs during an earlier period. Other reports have
published larger cumulative estimates.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">But PrimeAxis will not treat media estimates as audited
expenditure.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That would not meet the standard of evidence required for
this story.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Instead, we ask:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What has Nigeria actually spent on the floating dock since
2018?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The documentary answer is required.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">How much has been spent on:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Berthing?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Security?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Maintenance?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Insurance?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Technical inspections?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Towing?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Relocation?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Engineering works?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Site preparation?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Consultancy?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">PPP development?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Recommissioning?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Other associated costs?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And then:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What economic value has the asset generated since
acquisition?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That is the figure that should sit beside the expenditure.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Because the public conversation cannot remain only in the
language of what the dock will generate.&nbsp;</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">After eight years, Nigeria should also be able to state what
it has generated.</p>

<h3>WHAT ABOUT CAPITAL FLIGHT?<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">The economic argument for the floating dock has always been
linked to the cost of sending vessels abroad for repairs.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Industry stakeholders and media reports have cited
significant annual expenditure associated with foreign ship repairs.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">One widely reported figure has been approximately ₦180
billion annually.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">But PrimeAxis believes the number requires methodological
precision before it is presented as an established economic loss attributable
to the absence of this particular facility.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What exactly constitutes the estimated capital flight?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Is it only dry-docking expenditure?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Does it include repairs?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Spare parts?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Crew costs?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Classification?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Insurance?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Associated logistics?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And what portion is realistically capturable by a Nigerian
floating dock?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That last question matters.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Because it is one thing to say:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">“Nigeria loses ₦180 billion annually.”</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It is another and more useful&nbsp; to determine:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">“How much of that expenditure could this particular facility
realistically capture?”</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That is the number policymakers and investors need.</p>

<h3>THE TECHNICAL QUESTION: IS THE DOCK READY?<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">A dock that has spent years outside commercial operation
cannot simply be declared ready because it has been physically moved.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It must be technically ready.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That means questions around:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><ul><li>structural integrity;</li><li>machinery;</li><li>anchoring;</li><li>pumping systems;</li><li>electrical systems;</li><li>safety systems;</li><li>classification;</li><li>statutory surveys;</li><li>insurance;</li><li>commissioning;</li><li>operational procedures.</li></ul></p>





















<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA recognised the importance of technical
recommissioning when it announced in 2023 that Damen Shipyard, the original
manufacturer, would be invited to participate in the process.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The public record should now establish the outcome.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Has Damen inspected the dock?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What did the inspection find?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What repairs were recommended?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What has been completed?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What remains outstanding?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What is the current classification or certification status?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Who will certify the facility before commercial operations
begin?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">These are necessary questions for commercial confidence.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">A shipowner will require evidence that the facility is
technically suitable.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Insurance companies will require evidence.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Classification bodies will require evidence.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">International partners and financiers will require evidence.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The market will ultimately assess the facility on its
technical and commercial credibility.</p>

<h3>THE CAPACITY QUESTION ALSO NEEDS CLARITY<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">There is another issue that PrimeAxis believes should be
clarified publicly.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA stated in August 2023 that the Modular Floating Dock
had the capacity to handle vessels of up to 10,000 metric tonnes.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">However, the current ICRC project documentation lists the dock's
dry-dock lifting capacity as 5,000 tonnes, alongside a vessel docking capacity
of six metres draught.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">These figures should be reconciled technically.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Is the 5,000-tonne figure the dock's lifting capacity while
10,000 tonnes refers to the maximum vessel size or weight it can handle?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Is there another technical distinction between the two
figures?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What is the facility's current certified operating
specification?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The public deserves a single authoritative technical
specification.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">For investors and shipowners, capacity is not a press
release detail.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It determines the market the facility can serve.</p>

<h3>WHO IS ACTUALLY RUNNING THIS PROJECT?<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">This is another area where the institutional roles need to
be clearly stated.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Several institutions and companies have been associated with
the project:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA: owner and public-sector institution responsible for
the asset.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NPA: port infrastructure counterpart involved in the
Continental Shipyard site.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">ICRC: PPP regulatory institution.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">J. Marine Logistics: preferred bidder identified in the ICRC
project record for the management contract, with FEC approval recorded in
December 2022.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Melsmore Marine: technical partner involved in the site
preparation and relocation process. NIMASA formally handed the Continental
Shipyard site to Melsmore in 2023.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Damen Shipyard: original manufacturer identified by NIMASA
for possible involvement in recommissioning.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Each has a documented role in the project's history.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">But one question remains important for delivery:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Who is accountable for taking the floating dock into
commercial operation?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Not who is participating.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Not who attended a handover.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Not who was identified as a partner.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Who owns the delivery outcome?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That responsibility should be publicly identifiable and
measurable.&nbsp;</p>

<h3>THE “FINAL LAP” PROBLEM<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">This is where the chronology becomes important.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">In 2023, NIMASA described the takeover of Continental
Shipyard as the “final lap” in the deployment process.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">In August 2023, the agency handed the site to Melsmore and
said the technical partners would prepare for movement of the dock to
Continental Shipyard.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">In February 2024, NIMASA announced that the dock had been
moved to Standard Flour Mills in preparation for its movement to Continental
Shipyard.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The public record therefore shows a project that has passed
through several deployment stages over several years.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That does not establish that nothing has happened.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It establishes that the deployment process has been
prolonged.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That is why the next phase should be measured through
specific milestones rather than general assurances.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA should publish:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Site readiness date.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Technical inspection date.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Piling completion date.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Dock relocation date.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Recommissioning date.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Certification date.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">First vessel date.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Commercial operations date.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And the responsible institution for each milestone.</p>

<h3>WHAT NIGERIA SHOULD DO NOW<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">The answer is not to abandon the floating dock.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Nor should the government continue to describe its
deployment only in terms of future operations.&nbsp;</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria needs a Floating Dock Recovery and Commercialisation
Plan.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>1. Establish the asset’s exact current status</b><o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA should publish a concise status report covering:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Location.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Custody.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Physical condition.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Technical condition.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Certification.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Insurance.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Outstanding works.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Current operator.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">PPP status.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Expected commissioning date.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The country should not have to reconstruct this information
from scattered media reports.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>2. Complete an independent technical assessment</b><o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Damen's involvement should be substantive and documented.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The original manufacturer or another internationally
recognised marine engineering and classification authority should establish the
dock's current technical condition and what is required before commercial operations.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The outcome should be documented.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>3. Resolve the PPP decisively</b><o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The government should clarify the status of the concession.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">If J. Marine Logistics remains the concessionaire or
management partner, what is the current status of the arrangement?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">If the structure has changed, what changed?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">If commercial close has not been achieved, what prevented
it?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">If a new operating arrangement is being pursued, what is the
procurement and approval pathway?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The industry needs a clear commercial structure.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>4. Publish the actual financial exposure</b><o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Government should disclose the cumulative expenditure
associated with the asset since acquisition.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Not an estimate.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Not a newspaper claim.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The documented figure.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">This should include the cost of holding, maintaining,
moving, securing and preparing the dock.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">If some figures cannot be disclosed for legitimate
contractual reasons, government should explain why.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>5. Create a five year commercial plan</b><o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Before commissioning, Nigeria should know what the facility
is expected to do.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">How many vessels?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What categories?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What prices?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What utilisation rate?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What operating costs?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What staffing requirements?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What maintenance cycle?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What annual revenue?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What break-even point?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What regional market?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The business case should be measurable.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>6. Build the customer pipeline before commissioning</b><o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The dock should not open and then begin searching for
customers.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">NIMASA, shipowners, operators and maritime stakeholders
should develop a pipeline of vessels requiring maintenance.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The first years of operation should have identifiable
commercial targets.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>7. Build the wider ship repair ecosystem</b><o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The floating dock alone cannot create a maritime industrial
base.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria also needs:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Marine engineers.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Naval architects.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Welders and fabricators.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Marine electricians.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Machinists.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Spare parts suppliers.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Classification services.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Marine insurers.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Specialist financiers.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Training institutions.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The dock can serve as an anchor around which this ecosystem
develops.</p>

<h3>DO NOT MANDATE LOCAL DRY DOCKING WITHOUT BUILDING TRUST<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">There has been discussion around using cabotage policy to
encourage or require local dry docking.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The objective is understandable.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">But policy compulsion cannot substitute for competitiveness.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">If Nigeria wants shipowners to repair vessels locally, the
domestic facility must offer:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Safety.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Reliability.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Competitive pricing.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Predictable turnaround time.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Internationally credible certification.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Quality workmanship.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Access to spare parts.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Professional management.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">A shipowner should choose Nigeria because Nigeria is
commercially credible not simply because regulation leaves no alternative.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The stronger policy approach would combine appropriate local
content incentives with credible service delivery.</p>

<h3>THE FLOATING DOCK SHOULD BECOME A TEST OF GOVERNMENT
DELIVERY<o:p></o:p></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">This is ultimately why PrimeAxis is pursuing the story.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The floating dock is bigger than the dock.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It is a case study in how Nigeria manages strategic
infrastructure.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The country can acquire an asset.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">But can it integrate the asset?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Can agencies coordinate?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Can government move from acquisition to operation?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Can a PPP move from approval to commercial close?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Can technical partners move from appointment to measurable
delivery?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Can infrastructure become productive?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Can public expenditure produce measurable public value?<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">These are practical questions for Nigeria's blue-economy
ambitions.</p>

<h2>PRIMEAXIS INSIGHT<o:p></o:p></h2>

<p class="MsoNormal">The most important thing about the floating dock is not
simply that its deployment has taken years.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It is what the project demonstrates about the relationship
between infrastructure and economic activity.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria has no shortage of maritime opportunities.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has ships.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has cargo.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has ports.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has offshore activity.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has a cabotage regime.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has maritime training institutions.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has engineers and technicians.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has shipowners.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has a strategic coastline.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It has a large domestic and regional market.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The challenge is connecting those assets into productive
economic systems.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The floating dock was intended to be one of those
connections.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Instead, its public history shows an asset moving through
different locations, administrative processes, proposed partnerships and
technical preparations.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The public record also shows genuine attempts by NIMASA and
other institutions to move the project forward.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Both facts can be true.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That is why this story should not be reduced to an attack on
NIMASA.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The more useful institutional question is:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Why does Nigeria repeatedly find it easier to acquire
infrastructure than to make the infrastructure work?</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The answer may be found in the interfaces between:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Procurement and planning.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Public ownership and commercial management.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Political approval and technical execution.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">PPP approval and commercial close.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Infrastructure and the market it is supposed to serve.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The floating dock brings all of these interfaces into one
project.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And there is now an opportunity to establish a measurable
delivery process.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The latest phase of the project should therefore be measured
by outcomes.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria should be able to state:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Where the dock is.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What condition it is in.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">What remains to be done.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Who is responsible.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">How much more it will cost.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">When it will be certified.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">When the first vessel will enter.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And then Nigeria should do something even more important:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Measure whether the dock delivers.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Because eight years after acquisition, the relevant question
is no longer:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">“Can Nigeria build a floating dock?”</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It already has one.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The question is:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">“Can Nigeria turn a ₦50 billion maritime asset into a
functioning maritime industry?”<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That is the test.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">And the answer should not come from another press release.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">It should come from the first ship that enters the dock,
gets repaired, pays for the service and sails away.<o:p></o:p></p>
