<p>Nigeria has presented its investment proposition to Chinese investors at the 2026 Hainan Tour for Foreign-Invested Enterprises, highlighting opportunities across agriculture, energy, mining, manufacturing, technology, infrastructure and the maritime sector.</p><p>The presentation by Dr. Emi Membere-Otaji, 1st Deputy National President of the Nigerian Association of Chambers of Commerce, Industry, Mines and Agriculture (NACCIMA) and Chairman and CEO of ELSHCON Group of Companies, at the Nigerian Economic and Trade Exchange Session formed part of the Hainan-Shanxi Cooperation and International Investment Cooperation Promotion Conference, organised by the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT), Hainan Province, and the Hainan Chamber of Commerce.</p><p>Speaking on the theme, Why Invest in Nigeria, Membere-Otaji outlined the country's market potential, strategic geographical position, evolving regulatory environment and substantial investment requirements as reasons for Chinese businesses to consider expanding their presence in Nigeria.</p><p>He described Nigeria as a gateway to the 16 countries of West Africa and a strategic entry point into the African Continental Free Trade Area (AfCFTA), which connects businesses to a continental market of approximately 1.5 billion people.</p><p>With a population estimated at 250 million, he highlighted Nigeria's large domestic consumer base and youthful population as significant advantages for investors seeking market access and long-term business opportunities.</p><p>"There is no better time to invest in Nigeria," he told participants, pointing to improving macroeconomic conditions, regulatory developments and investment opportunities across critical sectors.</p><p><span style="color: rgb(21, 28, 46); font-family: &quot;Playfair Display&quot;, &quot;Playfair Display Fallback&quot;, Georgia, serif; font-size: 21px; font-weight: 600;">INVESTMENT CLIMATE AND REGULATORY REFORMS</span></p><p>A significant part of the presentation focused on changes in Nigeria's business environment.</p><p>Membere-Otaji cited regular economic data releases by the National Bureau of Statistics, increasing digitalisation of government processes and efforts to harmonise taxes and levies as developments capable of improving transparency and reducing the administrative burden on businesses.</p><p>He also highlighted investment incentives, including Pioneer Status tax holidays available to qualifying businesses, as instruments for attracting capital into priority sectors.</p><p>On the financial side, he pointed to regulated digital payment platforms and simplified cross-border payment processes as developments supporting commercial transactions.</p><p>Nigeria's port concession programme also featured prominently. Membere-Otaji described the participation of international terminal operators as evidence of opportunities created through public-private partnerships. He referenced global maritime and logistics companies, including MSC, APM Terminals and COSCO, in discussing international participation in Nigerian port operations and investments.</p><p>He further highlighted the establishment of the Nigeria Ports Economic Regulatory Agency (NPERA) under the NPERA Act, 2026, as an important development in the country's maritime investment environment.</p><p>The new statutory framework provides for economic regulation of port services, including tariffs and charges, licensing, service standards, competition and commercial dispute resolution.</p><p>For prospective investors in shipping, terminal operations, logistics and port-related services, effective implementation of the new regulatory framework will be important in strengthening investor confidence and protecting the interests of port users.</p><h2>EIGHT SECTORS IDENTIFIED FOR INVESTMENT</h2><p>Beyond the general investment climate, Membere-Otaji identified eight broad sectors where Nigeria's infrastructure and productive capacity gaps present opportunities for foreign and domestic investors.</p><p>Agriculture and agribusiness featured prominently, with opportunities in commercial fishing, trawling, aquaculture, crop production and agro-processing. He specifically mentioned cocoa, ginger and soya beans, alongside other agricultural commodities.</p><p>Power and renewable energy constituted another priority area, with opportunities spanning electricity generation, transmission and distribution.</p><p>In mining, he pointed to Nigeria's solid mineral deposits, including lithium and gold, as opportunities for investment in exploration, extraction and related value chains.</p><p>Industrial development and manufacturing also featured, particularly through industrial parks, free trade zones and economic zones that could support production and attract new capital.</p><p>Technology, healthcare and urban development formed another investment category. Opportunities included fintech, digital health, data centres, innovation hubs, pharmaceutical and diagnostic services, affordable housing and urban waste-to-wealth initiatives.</p><h2>MARITIME AND BLUE ECONOMY OPPORTUNITIES</h2><p>The maritime and transport sector received specific attention as an investment destination, with opportunities extending beyond traditional port operations.</p><p>Membere-Otaji identified vessel ownership, shipbuilding and repairs, port and terminal development, and logistics services as areas where investors could participate in Nigeria's maritime economy.</p><p>These opportunities reflect the wider potential of the blue economy, particularly in developing maritime infrastructure, expanding indigenous shipping capacity and strengthening the services that support regional and international trade.</p><p>Attracting investment into these areas is important given the capital requirements associated with maritime infrastructure, vessel acquisition, ship maintenance, cargo movement and integrated logistics.</p><p>The establishment of NPERA also creates an opportunity to strengthen the institutional environment within which these investments operate.</p><p>Oil and gas was another major component of the presentation. Membere-Otaji highlighted opportunities across the upstream, midstream and downstream segments, including petrochemicals, fertiliser production, gas pipelines, liquefied natural gas, compressed natural gas and related construction and energy services.</p><p>He also referenced the Federal Government's Decade of Gas initiative and energy transition policies as frameworks supporting investment across the gas value chain.</p><p>Infrastructure development and maintenance completed the eight priority areas, reflecting Nigeria's substantial requirements for investment in the physical assets needed to support economic expansion.</p><h2>LOCAL PARTERSHIPS AS AN ENTRY POINT</h2><p>Membere-Otaji emphasised the importance of reliable local partnerships in helping foreign investors understand and navigate Nigeria's business environment.</p><p>He identified the Nigerian Investment Promotion Commission, Nigerian Export Promotion Council and relevant state investment agencies as important institutional gateways.</p><p>He also highlighted the Nigerian Association of Chambers of Commerce, Industry, Mines and Agriculture (NACCIMA), describing its private sector network and relationships with government institutions as valuable resources for prospective investors.</p><p>He noted that Chinese businesses already operate across several Nigerian economic sectors, with some establishing new ventures and others investing in existing enterprises to expand their operations.</p><p>Concluding his presentation, he encouraged Chinese investors to explore Nigeria's opportunities, assuring them that credible local business partners were available to support market entry and business development.</p><p>"As a local investor in Nigeria, I can confidently tell you, come in, the coast is open, the gaps are huge, return on investments attractive," he said.</p><p>In his reflection on the engagement, Membere-Otaji expressed satisfaction with the interest generated among Chinese participants, describing the opportunity to present Nigeria's investment proposition as a valuable platform for strengthening bilateral business relations.</p><h2>PRIMEAXIS INSIGHT</h2><p>Nigeria's investment proposition is compelling in its breadth, but the challenge is translating market potential into bankable projects, productive investments and sustainable returns.</p><p>A large population and access to the African market create commercial possibilities, but investors will ultimately assess regulatory predictability, infrastructure reliability, access to finance, operating costs, security and the ease of doing business.</p><p>The maritime sector illustrates this distinction clearly. Nigeria has international terminal operators, an established port concession framework and a new statutory economic regulator. Yet these institutional and commercial developments must translate into more competitive ports, efficient cargo movement, transparent charges and stronger integration with the country's wider transport and logistics infrastructure.</p><p>Similarly, opportunities in agriculture, manufacturing, mining and energy require more than expressions of investment interest. They demand credible project preparation, infrastructure, technology transfer, reliable local partnerships and a business environment capable of supporting long-term operations.</p><p>For Nigeria, engagements such as the Hainan investment conference are important beyond the immediate opportunity to attract foreign capital. They provide a platform to communicate economic priorities, connect project promoters with international investors and build partnerships capable of strengthening domestic productive capacity.</p><p>Ultimately, the success of Nigeria's investment drive will not be measured by the number of opportunities presented or the interest generated in conference halls. It will be measured by the quality of partnerships established, the capital committed and the projects successfully delivered.</p>
