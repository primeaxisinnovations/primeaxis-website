<p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria cannot physically police more than 4,000 kilometres
of land borders. Recognising this reality, the Nigeria Customs Service (NCS) is
shifting from a manpower-intensive enforcement model to one driven by
intelligence, technology and strategic partnerships—a transition that could
redefine border governance in Africa's largest economy.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><o:p>&nbsp;</o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Speaking on the Nigerian Television Authority's Good Morning
Nigeria, the National Public Relations Officer of the NCS, Deputy Comptroller
Abdullahi Maiwada, outlined what is emerging as a significant evolution in the
Service's border management strategy. The approach combines intelligence-led
operations, geospatial technology, interagency collaboration and community
engagement to strengthen border security while facilitating legitimate trade.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The shift reflects a growing recognition that conventional
enforcement alone is no longer sufficient to secure one of Africa's largest and
most complex border networks.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal"><img src="https://n0jyvquhgs2welj7.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/uploads/1783863912623-f08bb928-3-sitted.webp" alt=""></p><p class="MsoNormal"><b><br></b></p><p class="MsoNormal"><b>Intelligence Becomes the First Line of Defence</b></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">At the heart of the new strategy is an intelligence-first
operating model.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Rather than attempting to maintain a constant physical
presence across thousands of kilometres of difficult terrain, the Customs
Service is prioritising intelligence gathering, risk analysis and technology to
identify and disrupt illicit trade before it reaches critical border corridors.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><i>"You can't be everywhere at a time,"</i> Maiwada
observed, explaining that the Service is increasingly deploying
intelligence-driven operations to maximise limited operational resources.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That philosophy has already informed specialised initiatives
such as Operation Whirlwind, established to curb the smuggling of petroleum
products across Nigeria's borders.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">According to Maiwada, the operation deliberately maintains a
low public profile, allowing intelligence—not visibility—to drive enforcement
outcomes.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The approach reflects the evolution of customs administrations
worldwide, where data analytics, risk profiling and targeted interventions have
become more effective than blanket physical inspections.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><o:p>&nbsp;</o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>Geospatial Technology Moves from Concept to Deployment</b></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">One of the most significant announcements was the Service's
expansion of geospatial technology as part of its border management strategy.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Maiwada disclosed that Customs has already geolocated
operational locations across Nigeria's South-West border corridor and is
preparing to extend the initiative to other parts of the country.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">He also revealed ongoing collaboration with the Beninese
Customs Administration at the Seme border to jointly map border locations,
enabling both countries to better monitor the movement of goods across their
shared frontier.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">If successfully implemented, geospatial mapping could
significantly improve Customs' ability to monitor border activities, optimise
operational deployment and strengthen surveillance across traditionally
difficult border corridors.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">For an agency responsible for managing thousands of
kilometres of land borders, location intelligence could become one of its most
valuable operational assets.</p><p class="MsoNormal"><o:p><img src="https://n0jyvquhgs2welj7.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/uploads/1783864465506-61c7d904-ev.webp" alt=""></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b><br></b></p><p class="MsoNormal"><b>Border Communities Become Part of the Security Architecture</b><o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The strategy extends beyond technology.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Maiwada stressed that sustainable border security depends on
the cooperation of communities living along Nigeria's borders.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Without local trust and timely intelligence, enforcement
agencies face significant limitations regardless of operational capacity.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Recognising this reality, the Service is deepening community
engagement through its Customs Cares initiative while also assuring border
communities that they will receive consideration in the ongoing recruitment
exercise.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The approach represents an important shift in thinking—from
treating border communities solely as enforcement environments to recognising
them as strategic partners in protecting legitimate trade, improving
intelligence gathering and strengthening national security.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><o:p>&nbsp;</o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>Intelligence Requires Collaboration</b></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Another defining feature of the emerging strategy is
interoperability.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Maiwada emphasised that effective border governance
increasingly depends on seamless cooperation among customs administrations,
domestic security agencies and international partners.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">According to him, the Service is strengthening
intelligence-sharing arrangements with agencies including the Nigeria Police
Force, the Department of State Services, the Nigeria Immigration Service, the
National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA), the National Agency for Food and
Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) and INTERPOL.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Such collaboration has already contributed to several
high-profile interceptions, including the recovery of stolen vehicles and the
seizure of illicit drugs and endangered wildlife products.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">In an era of increasingly interconnected supply chains and
transnational crime, intelligence sharing is becoming as important as physical
enforcement.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><o:p>&nbsp;<img src="https://n0jyvquhgs2welj7.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/uploads/1783865215940-6324c779-chatgpt-image-jul-12-2026-03-06-21-pm.webp" alt=""></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>Beyond Enforcement</b></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">A notable aspect of Maiwada's presentation was his emphasis
on balancing enforcement with persuasion.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Beyond seizures and arrests, the Service is investing in
stakeholder engagement and strategic communication to encourage voluntary
compliance with customs regulations.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The philosophy recognises that sustainable compliance is
more likely when traders understand both their regulatory obligations and the
broader economic value of lawful trade.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Maiwada described the evolving approach as one built on
three complementary pillars—Enforcement, Cohesion and Persuasion—which together
provide a more comprehensive framework for managing Nigeria's complex border
environment.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><o:p>&nbsp;</o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><b>What This Means for Trade</b></p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The Customs Service's evolving strategy comes at a time when
Nigeria is seeking to strengthen trade competitiveness while enhancing national
security.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Efficient border management has become increasingly
important as regional commerce expands under the African Continental Free Trade
Area (AfCFTA) and governments seek to reduce logistics costs, improve supply
chain efficiency and protect legitimate trade from illicit activities.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">For Customs, success will ultimately be measured not only by
seizures and arrests but also by its ability to facilitate compliant trade,
improve revenue collection and build confidence in Nigeria's border management
system.<br><br></p><p class="MsoNormal"><b>PrimeAxis Insight</b></p><p class="MsoNormal">The shift outlined by the Nigeria Customs Service reflects
more than an operational adjustment—it signals a strategic evolution in how
border security is being conceived.</p><p class="MsoNormal">As illicit trade networks become increasingly sophisticated
and regional commerce expands under the African Continental Free Trade Area,
the competitive advantage will belong not to the countries with the most
checkpoints, but to those with the best intelligence, strongest institutional
collaboration and smartest application of technology.</p><p class="MsoNormal">For Nigeria, the success of this strategy will ultimately be
measured not only by enforcement outcomes, but by safer borders, faster trade
flows, stronger revenue protection and greater confidence in the country's
logistics ecosystem.</p><p class="MsoNormal">If sustained, this intelligence-led approach could become
one of the defining pillars of Nigeria's customs modernisation agenda and a
model for border governance across Africa.<o:p></o:p></p>
