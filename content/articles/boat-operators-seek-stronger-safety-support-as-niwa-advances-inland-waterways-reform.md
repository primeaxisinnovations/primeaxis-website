<p class="MsoNormal">Nigeria's ambition to unlock the economic potential of its
inland waterways will depend not only on infrastructure and policy reforms but
also on the safety standards that underpin public confidence in water
transportation.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">That message emerged as the Association of Tourist Boat
Operators and Water Transporters of Nigeria (ATBOWATON) called on the National
Inland Waterways Authority (NIWA) to strengthen support for waterway safety
through increased provision of life jackets and other critical safety
equipment.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The appeal was made during a courtesy visit to the NIWA
Abuja Area/Liaison Office, where the association congratulated the Acting
Managing Director, Alhaji Umar Yusuf Girei, and the Area Manager, Mrs. Aisha
Eri, on their recent appointments.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Beyond a Courtesy Visit.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Although the visit was ceremonial, it provided a platform
for operators to raise operational issues affecting Nigeria's inland water
transport sector.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Speaking on behalf of the association, ATBOWATON National
President, Dr. Gani Tarzan Balogun, commended NIWA for its sustained engagement
with operators and its growing emphasis on improving safety across the nation's
waterways.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">He cited the Authority's interventions in parts of the
South-West as evidence of progress but noted that the shortage of life jackets
remains one of the sector's most pressing challenges.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">According to him, expanding access to life-saving equipment
is essential to protecting passengers and crews while supporting the continued
growth of inland water transportation.</p>

<h3>Safety as Economic Infrastructure</h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">The appeal reinforces a broader reality: the expansion of
inland water transport cannot be sustained without credible safety systems.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">As governments increasingly promote waterways as an
alternative to congested road networks, the sector's growth will depend on
strong regulatory oversight, operational discipline and adequate safety
equipment.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">For operators, life jackets are more than compliance
requirements—they are fundamental infrastructure for building trust in water
transportation.</p>

<h3>Unlocking the Potential of Inland Waterways</h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">Dr. Balogun also emphasised the need to improve the navigability
of Nigeria's inland waterways, arguing that a more efficient water transport
network would ease pressure on the country's overstretched roads while
providing safer and more cost-effective options for passengers and cargo
movement.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">His observations align with Nigeria's broader blue economy
agenda, which recognises inland waterways as strategic assets for improving
connectivity, reducing logistics costs and supporting economic growth.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Realising that potential, however, will require sustained investment
in infrastructure, safety and institutional capacity.</p>

<h3>NIWA Reaffirms Commitment</h3>

<p class="MsoNormal">Responding, Acting Managing Director Alhaji Umar Yusuf Girei
thanked the association for the visit and assured members that many of the
issues raised were already receiving attention.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">He noted that the Authority had begun implementing measures
to address key operational concerns and reaffirmed NIWA's commitment to
strengthening the inland waterways sector.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">Also speaking, Abuja Area Manager Mrs. Aisha Eri described the
engagement as timely and encouraged ATBOWATON members to deploy their
operational experience in supporting the development of water transportation
within the Federal Capital Territory.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">She expressed confidence that closer collaboration between
regulators and operators would contribute to safer and more efficient inland
water transport services.<o:p></o:p></p>

<h2>PrimeAxis Insight</h2>

<p class="MsoNormal">The engagement between ATBOWATON and NIWA underscores an
important principle of maritime development: successful water transport systems
are built on more than vessels and waterways. They require an ecosystem that
combines effective regulation, navigable channels, operational professionalism
and uncompromising safety standards.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">As Nigeria seeks to expand multimodal transportation and
deepen its blue economy, investments in waterway safety should be viewed as
investments in economic productivity. A transport system earns public
confidence not simply by moving more people and cargo, but by doing so safely,
consistently and efficiently.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal">The strength of Nigeria's inland waterways sector will
ultimately be measured not by the number of boats on its rivers, but by the
confidence with which people choose to use them.<o:p></o:p></p>
