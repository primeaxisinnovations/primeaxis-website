<p>The Nigeria Customs Service (NCS), Apapa Area Command, generated ₦302.95 billion in October 2026, recording a 7.13 per cent increase over the ₦282.79 billion collected in September, as the Customs Area Controller, Comptroller Murtala Mu’azu, prioritises stakeholder collaboration, operational efficiency and trade facilitation in his first month in office.</p><p>The October collection of ₦302,952,570,769.24 represents an increase of approximately ₦20.16 billion over the preceding month. It also marks another monthly collection above the ₦300 billion threshold, following the Command’s record performance of over ₦323 billion in July 2026.</p><p>Mu’azu assumed duty on September 29, 2026, and has since focused on improving operational efficiency and service delivery in line with the Comptroller General of Customs, Bashir Adewale Adeniyi’s policy direction of Consolidation, Collaboration and Innovation.</p><h2>STAKEHOLDER COLLABORATION AND TRADE FACILITATION</h2><p>A key feature of the new administration has been its engagement with stakeholders across the port community.</p><p>On October 5, Mu’azu convened a meeting involving importers, exporters, licensed Customs agents, shipping companies and terminal operators to address operational challenges affecting port users.</p><p>Discussions focused on delays in Demand Notices, cargo clearance bottlenecks and other concerns affecting port operations.</p><p><img src="https://encrypted-tbn0.gstatic.com/images?q=tbn:ANd9GcTbltMKAkfRYHhvQ4q7Whdakspg6V65ViV4Zg&amp;s" alt=""><br></p><p>The Area Controller has also undertaken courtesy visits to strategic partners and stakeholders, seeking to strengthen institutional relationships, improve mutual understanding and encourage the collaboration required for a more efficient business environment.</p><p>These engagements recognise the importance of coordination among the various actors involved in port operations. For traders, delays arising from administrative inefficiencies and weak coordination can increase transaction costs and affect cargo movement.</p><p>The effectiveness of these engagements will ultimately depend on how identified challenges are addressed and whether improvements become evident in everyday port operations.</p><h2>STREAMLINING TRANSIRE AND CARGO EXAMINATIONS</h2><p>Another area receiving attention is the processing of Transire, which the Command is reviewing to eliminate unnecessary bureaucratic bottlenecks, improve interdepartmental coordination and reduce processing time.</p><p>Mu’azu said the initiative was intended to ensure that the various offices involved discharged their responsibilities more efficiently while maintaining the necessary compliance and control measures.</p><p>He emphasised that traders should not suffer the consequences of avoidable administrative lapses or delays, adding that operational challenges must be identified and addressed at their root.</p><p>The Area Controller has also directed that cargo examinations be conducted strictly in accordance with established procedures, requisite documentation and authorisation.</p><p>He cautioned officers against unwarranted, repetitive or unnecessary checks capable of frustrating legitimate business transactions, while directing them to discharge their responsibilities promptly and professionally.</p><p>The directives seek to improve cargo clearance and turnaround time without compromising compliance, revenue protection or national security.</p><p>The Command is also strengthening coordination among relevant departments and port stakeholders to address operational bottlenecks affecting cargo movement.</p><p>These measures are particularly relevant at Apapa, where delays in cargo clearance can increase storage and demurrage costs, disrupt supply chains and affect the commercial operations of importers and exporters.</p><p>The challenge is to ensure that efforts to accelerate clearance do not weaken risk assessment, enforcement or the statutory responsibilities of the Service under the Nigeria Customs Service Act, 2023.</p><h2>ENFORCEMENT AGAINST ILLICIT TRADE</h2><p>Alongside its trade facilitation initiatives, the Command reported the interception of 437 sachets of Cannabis Sativa weighing a combined 392.5 kilogrammes in two separate containers during the period under review.</p><p>The seizures comprised 125 kilogrammes in container number MSKU0486747 and 267.5 kilogrammes in container number TEMU6149358.</p><p>The combined Duty Paid Value of the seized consignments stands at ₦196,131,606.82.</p><p>According to the Command, the consignments were jointly examined with relevant sister agencies, underscoring its commitment to preventing the use of Nigeria’s maritime gateway for illicit trade and protecting public health and national security.</p><p>The interceptions reinforce the enforcement responsibility of Customs within the port system, particularly as the Service seeks to facilitate legitimate trade while preventing prohibited goods from entering or passing through the country.</p><h2>THE TEST OF SUSTAINABLE REFORM</h2><p>Mu’azu’s first month presents an early indication of the priorities his administration intends to pursue. The October revenue performance provides a strong starting point, while the emphasis on stakeholder engagement, Transire processing and disciplined cargo examinations addresses operational concerns affecting port users.</p><p>However, sustaining these measures will require consistent implementation and demonstrable improvements in service delivery.</p><p>The Command will need to establish whether its review of Transire processing reduces administrative delays, whether cargo examinations become more predictable and whether stakeholder engagement produces practical solutions to persistent operational challenges.</p><h2>PRIMEAXIS INSIGHT</h2><p>Apapa Customs is beginning a new administrative phase with strong revenue performance and an expressed commitment to operational reform. The more consequential test, however, is whether these priorities translate into a more efficient trading environment.</p><p>Revenue generation and trade facilitation should reinforce each other. An efficient clearance system can encourage legitimate trade, improve compliance and strengthen government revenue, while effective enforcement protects the economy against illicit transactions.</p><p>Mu’azu's emphasis on reducing avoidable delays and improving coordination addresses an important part of this relationship. But stakeholder meetings and administrative directives are only the beginning. Their value must ultimately be measured by the experience of businesses operating through the port.</p><p>Will traders experience shorter processing times? Will unnecessary cargo examinations decline? Will the operational challenges identified by stakeholders be resolved? And can Apapa sustain strong revenue collections while improving the ease of doing business?</p><p>For Nigeria’s busiest port complex, the objective must extend beyond collecting more revenue. It must be to create a Customs environment where legitimate trade moves efficiently, compliance is predictable and enforcement remains effective.</p><p>That is the standard against which the new administration’s reform agenda should be measured.</p>
