<h2>Who Really Captures the Value?</h2><p>Africa accounts for a significant share of global maritime trade.</p><p>More than 90% of the continent's international trade moves by sea.</p><p>Ships call at African ports every day.</p><p>African commodities supply global industries.</p><p>African consumers drive growing import demand.</p><p>Yet one uncomfortable reality remains.</p><p>Africa moves vast volumes of cargo.</p><p>But captures only a fraction of the value generated by that trade.</p><p>Because across much of the maritime value chain, ownership, financing, transport, insurance, and logistics services remain concentrated outside the continent.</p><p>This is the hidden cost of foreign dependence.</p><h3>1. Trade Does Not Automatically Create Wealth</h3><p>Trade creates opportunity.</p><p>But value is captured by those who control the value chain.</p><p>Across much of Africa, foreign shipping operators continue to carry the overwhelming majority of international cargo moving to and from the continent.</p><p>As a result, a substantial share of freight payments, marine insurance premiums, vessel charter revenues, financing income, and specialised maritime service revenues flows outside African economies.</p><p>The cargo may be African.</p><p>The commercial value generated by transporting it often is not.</p><h3>2. The Cost Extends Far Beyond Freight</h3><p>The financial impact extends far beyond shipping costs alone.</p><p>It includes:</p><p><ul><li>Freight payments</li><li>Marine insurance</li><li>Vessel chartering</li><li>Ship management</li><li>Port agency services</li><li>Technical services</li><li>Overseas repairs and dry-docking</li><li>Maritime finance</li></ul></p><p>Each represents value that could increasingly be retained within African economies as indigenous maritime capability expands.</p><h3>3. Dependence Creates Vulnerability</h3><p>Reliance on external shipping capacity also creates strategic risk.</p><p>Global disruptions—from pandemics to geopolitical tensions and major shipping route disruptions—demonstrate how quickly supply chains can be affected.</p><p>When shipping capacity tightens, carriers naturally allocate vessels according to commercial priorities.</p><p>For many African importers and exporters, that can mean:</p><p>Longer transit times.</p><p>Reduced service frequency.</p><p>Higher freight rates.</p><p>Container shortages.</p><p>Greater supply chain uncertainty.</p><p>The lesson is clear.</p><p>Resilience depends not only on efficient ports, but also on stronger indigenous maritime capability.</p><h3>4. Flag Registration Is Not the Same as Maritime Ownership</h3><p>Africa hosts some of the world's largest ship registries.</p><p>Yet ship registration alone does not guarantee domestic economic value.</p><p>Owning a ship registry differs fundamentally from owning vessels, operating fleets, employing seafarers, financing ships, or managing global shipping services.</p><p>True maritime value comes from controlling a greater share of the commercial ecosystem.</p><h3>5. From Dependence to Value Capture</h3><p>Reducing foreign dependence does not mean replacing international partnerships.</p><p>Global shipping will always be international.</p><p>The objective is different.</p><p>Africa must increase its participation in the highest-value segments of the maritime economy.</p><p>That includes:</p><p><ul><li>Indigenous shipping companies</li><li>Ship finance</li><li>Marine insurance</li><li>Shipbuilding and repair</li><li>Maritime technology</li><li>Logistics services</li><li>Port operations</li><li>Maritime data and intelligence</li><li>Skilled maritime professionals</li></ul></p><p>The more capability Africa develops, the more value it retains.</p><h3>6. The Strategic Opportunity</h3><p>The African Continental Free Trade Area offers a platform to rethink how maritime value is created across the continent.</p><p>More regional trade.</p><p>Stronger indigenous shipping.</p><p>Integrated logistics corridors.</p><p>Digital trade facilitation.</p><p>Regional maritime financing.</p><p>Coordinated industrial development.</p><p>Together, these can reduce costs, strengthen resilience, and increase Africa's share of the value generated by its own trade.</p><h3>FINAL THOUGHT</h3><p>Africa does not simply need to trade more.</p><p>It must capture more value from the trade it already generates.</p><p>Because the greatest opportunity is not only moving African cargo.</p><p>It is owning more of the systems that move it.</p><p>Ships.</p><p>Finance.</p><p>Insurance.</p><p>Technology.</p><p>Logistics.</p><p>Industrial capability.</p><p>And maritime intelligence.</p><p>Because in global shipping, those who control the value chain capture the greatest value.</p><p>The future of African maritime development will be determined not only by how much trade the continent generates—</p><p><b><i>But by how much of that trade Africa ultimately owns</i></b>.</p>
