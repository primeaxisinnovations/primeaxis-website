<p class="MsoNormal">The Nigerian Ports Consultative Council
(NPCC) has intensified its push for seamless, transparent and predictable port
operations, calling for deeper digitalisation, stronger inter-agency
coordination and streamlined trade processes to improve Nigeria’s competitiveness
in regional and international trade.</p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">At its quarterly meeting held at the
Federal Palace Hotel, Lagos, on September 17, 2026, stakeholders examined
measures to simplify cross-border trade, reduce operational bottlenecks and
improve the ease of doing business at Nigerian ports.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The meeting, themed “Simplifying
Cross-Border Trade and Enhancing Ease of Doing Business at Our Ports,” brought
together government agencies and maritime industry stakeholders to examine
challenges affecting cargo movement, trade facilitation and port efficiency.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<h2><span lang="EN-US">Coordinating the Port Ecosystem<o:p></o:p></span></h2>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Chairman of the NPCC, Mr Bolaji Sunmola,
said Nigeria’s ambition to position its ports as a hub for West and Central
Africa would remain difficult to achieve without addressing infrastructure and
operational bottlenecks.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">He said the Council provides a platform for
stakeholders to bring operational challenges directly to relevant government
agencies and develop practical solutions.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Sunmola called for open engagement among
agencies, urging them to explain their activities, identify persistent
challenges and present solutions.The approach places coordinated
problem-solving at the centre of the Council’s efforts to improve port
efficiency.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<h2><span lang="EN-US">Digitalisation and System Integration<o:p></o:p></span></h2>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Vice Chairman of the NPCC, Mrs Jean Chiazor
Anishere, SAN, called for deeper digitalisation and automation to reduce
duplication, improve transparency and strengthen compliance.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">She identified an integrated Port Community
System, inter-agency data sharing, electronic documentation and risk-based
inspections as important measures for improving cargo movement.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Anishere stressed that cross-border trade
requires coordination among government agencies, port operators, shipping
lines, terminal operators, freight forwarders, customs brokers, financial
institutions and traders.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">She also noted that fragmented or
unnecessarily prolonged processes increase costs for businesses and the trading
public.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The objective is therefore not simply to
digitise individual processes, but to improve how information and regulatory
procedures connect across the wider port ecosystem.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<h2><span lang="EN-US">The seven-day clearance target<o:p></o:p></span></h2>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Director-General of the Nigerian Maritime
Administration and Safety Agency (NIMASA), Dr Dayo Mobereola, said efficient,
transparent and predictable port operations were central to Nigeria’s maritime
economy and its competitiveness under the African Continental Free Trade Area
(AfCFTA).<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Mobereola, represented at the meeting by
the Director of Shipping Development, Mr Abdullahi Salihu, said delays increase
costs, weaken confidence and undermine the competitiveness of Nigerian
businesses across African markets.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">He said market size and geographical
position alone would not guarantee cargo throughput without reliable,
transparent and efficient trade corridors.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">According to him, the National Single
Window, launched on March 27, 2026, is designed to unify import and export
documentation, reduce duplication and support a reduction in cargo clearance
time from 21 days to seven days.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">He cautioned, however, that technology
alone would not deliver the target.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Accurate data, clear service standards and
disciplined cooperation among agencies operating within the port and border
environment would also be required.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">&nbsp;</span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The seven-day objective therefore depends
on how effectively digital systems connect with regulatory processes and
operational procedures.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<h2><span lang="EN-US">MEMS and Regulatory Visibility<o:p></o:p></span></h2>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">NIMASA has also deployed its Maritime
Electronic Management System (MEMS), a separate regulatory digital system
designed to improve visibility across maritime operations.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The Agency says MEMS provides digital
traceability of vessel movements, operational records and regulatory
interactions, while automated alerts, smart invoicing and centralised data
integration are intended to support faster regulatory decisions.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The system also provides digital tracking
for services such as waste reception, including time-stamped records and
billing trails, while supporting monitoring of marine pollution incidents.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The significance of MEMS within the wider
port efficiency conversation is its potential to improve regulatory visibility
and reduce dependence on fragmented manual processes.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Its effectiveness, however, will depend on
how well it interfaces with the broader trade and port operating environment.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<h2><span lang="EN-US">Ports and National Competitiveness<o:p></o:p></span></h2>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Anishere said Nigerian ports must be viewed
not merely as cargo gateways but as critical instruments of national economic
development.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">She highlighted their impact on goods
prices, business competitiveness, investor attraction and the performance of
Nigeria’s international trade.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Her remarks place port efficiency within a
wider economic context, where the cost and predictability of moving cargo
directly affect businesses and the competitiveness of Nigerian trade.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">She also identified efficient dispute
resolution mechanisms, particularly maritime and commercial arbitration, as
important to investor confidence and participation in international trade.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The Council, she said, would continue
engaging stakeholders and ensure that its recommendations are brought before
the Ministry of Marine and Blue Economy.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<h2><span lang="EN-US">Trade Facilitation and Export Competitiveness<o:p></o:p></span></h2>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The Nigerian Export Promotion Council
(NEPC) also identified the simplification of cross-border trade and
improvements in the ease of doing business at ports as important to
diversifying non-oil exports and supporting entrepreneurs.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The Managing Director of NEPC, Mrs. Nonye
Ayeni, represented by Assistant Director Mrs Ronke Olaiye, said the Council
would continue to amplify exporters’ concerns in efforts to improve trade
facilitation.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The position reinforces the connection
between efficient port operations, export competitiveness and Nigeria’s wider
economic diversification objectives.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<h3><span lang="EN-US">PrimeAxis Insight<o:p></o:p></span></h3>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">Nigeria’s port efficiency challenge is
increasingly a question of system integration, coordination and execution.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The NPCC’s push for seamless port
operations reflects a recognition that trade competitiveness depends on how
effectively the institutions and processes governing cargo movement work
together.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">An integrated Port Community System,
electronic documentation and inter-agency data sharing could reduce duplication
and improve the movement of information across the trade ecosystem.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The National Single Window provides a
framework for integrating trade documentation, while NIMASA’s MEMS provides
greater digital visibility across maritime regulatory activities.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">These are different systems addressing
different parts of the operating environment. Their broader value will depend
on how effectively they work alongside the other institutions and processes
involved in cargo movement.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The seven-day cargo clearance target
provides a measurable benchmark for assessing progress. But achieving it will
require more than technology.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">It will require agencies to operate with
clearer service standards, reliable data, coordinated procedures and greater
predictability for traders.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">For Nigeria’s ambition to strengthen its
position in regional and international trade, port efficiency must therefore be
measured across the entire trade journey rather than within individual
institutions.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The real test of a seamless port is whether
cargo moves with fewer delays, greater certainty and lower avoidable costs.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">That is where the impact of the NPCC’s
coordination efforts, the National Single Window and NIMASA’s digital reforms
will ultimately be measured.<o:p></o:p></span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal"><span lang="EN-US">The question now is whether these
initiatives can translate into a trade environment where efficiency is not
simply a policy objective, but a consistent operational reality.<o:p></o:p></span></p>
