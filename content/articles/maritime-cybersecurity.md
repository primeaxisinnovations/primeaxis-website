<h2>The Invisible Threat to Trade</h2><p><span style="color: rgb(21, 28, 46); font-family: &quot;Playfair Display&quot;, &quot;Playfair Display Fallback&quot;, Georgia, serif; font-size: 18px; font-weight: 600;">Why Africa’s
Digital Maritime Transformation Must Be Matched by Cyber Resilience</span></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Maritime
security is too often treated as a response to disruption rather than an
architecture for preventing it.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">For years, the
maritime security conversation has intensified after a vessel is threatened, an
accident occurs, operations are disrupted or the financial and human costs
become impossible to ignore.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">By then,
security has already become crisis management.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">But
digitalisation is changing the equation.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A port no
longer needs to be physically attacked to experience operational disruption.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A vessel does
not need to be boarded. Cargo does not need to be stolen.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Disrupt the systems
that coordinate them, and trade can be disrupted without touching the physical
infrastructure itself.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That is the
invisible threat emerging alongside Africa’s digital maritime transformation.<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And it raises a
more difficult question:<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Is Africa
building a more digital maritime economy faster than it is building the
resilience<o:p></o:p></p>

<p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">needed to
protect it?</p><h3>The Port is no longer only Physical</h3><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">For decades,
maritime security was largely understood through physical infrastructure.&nbsp;</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Gates.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Fences.&nbsp;</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Cranes.&nbsp;</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Patrols.&nbsp;</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Surveillance.&nbsp;</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Access control.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That definition
is no longer sufficient.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A modern port
is simultaneously a physical facility and a digital ecosystem.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Terminal
operating systems, vessel scheduling<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">platforms,
cargo-management systems, automated equipment, access-control systems,
communications networks, customs interfaces, payment platforms and third-party
technology services can all form part of the operational chain.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The cranes may
still stand.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The vessels may
still be alongside. The gates may still be secure.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">But if the
digital systems coordinating the movement of cargo and information become
unavailable, the port’s ability to function can be severely compromised.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The
vulnerability is no longer simply inside the port. It is also in the network of
systems on which the port depends.<o:p></o:p></p><p>



















</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And that
network extends far beyond the port itself.<br><o:p></o:p></p><h3>The Attack Surface is The Maritime Ecosystem</h3><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A single
maritime transaction can involve a shipowner, charterer, port authority,
terminal operator, customs administration, freight forwarder, logistics
provider, insurer, bank and multiple technology vendors.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Information
moves continuously between them. So does risk.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A company may
have strong internal cybersecurity and still be exposed through a<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">third-party provider.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A port may
secure its own infrastructure while depending on an external platform.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A logistics
company may protect its network while exchanging data with a less secure
partner. This exposes a weakness in the way cyber risk is often understood.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">We ask:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Is this
organisation secure?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">But maritime
trade does not operate as isolated organisations.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It operates as
an interconnected system.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">So the more
important questions are:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Who owns the
risk when a critical dependency<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">sits outside
the organisation?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Who is responsible
when the vulnerability exists between two systems rather than inside one?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Who sees the
full threat picture when each institution sees only its own part of the
network?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And ultimately:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">How resilient
is the maritime ecosystem when one of its critical digital dependencies fails?<o:p></o:p></p><p>

































</p><p>That is a much
bigger question than cybersecurity compliance.<br><span style="color: rgb(21, 28, 46); font-family: &quot;Playfair Display&quot;, &quot;Playfair Display Fallback&quot;, Georgia, serif; font-size: 18px; font-weight: 600;">Cyber Risk Is Now Trade Risk</span></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The economic
consequences of a cyber incident do not stop at the IT department.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A compromised
cargo system can delay clearance.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A disruption to
terminal systems can create congestion.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A failure in
vessel-management or communications systems can affect operations. A
compromised payment platform can interrupt transactions.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A disruption to
logistics systems can create bottlenecks beyond the port and into inland supply
chains.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">In other words:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Digital
disruption can become physical and<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">economic
disruption.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That creates a
direct relationship between cybersecurity and trade resilience.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The relevant
question is not simply:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Was data
stolen? It is:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Could trade
continue? That distinction is critical.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A cyber
incident that exposes confidential<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">information is
serious.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A cyber
incident that prevents a port, vessel, terminal or logistics network from
functioning can become a trade event.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And once the
consequences reach cargo flows, vessel schedules, supply chains and financial
transactions, cybersecurity has moved firmly<o:p></o:p></p><p>































</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">into the
territory of economic security.<o:p></o:p></p><h3>African's Digital Ambition Has a Risk Equation</h3><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Africa has
compelling reasons to accelerate maritime digitalisation.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Digital systems
can reduce paperwork, improve cargo visibility, strengthen coordination, lower
transaction costs and support better operational decisions.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Artificial
intelligence and data analytics could further transform forecasting, logistics
and maritime risk management.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">These are
opportunities Africa should pursue. But digitalisation changes the risk
equation.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Every new
connection creates another potential<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">exposure.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Every automated
process creates another system that must remain available.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Every new
platform creates another dependency. Every third-party provider creates another
part of the operational chain that may sit outside the direct control of the
organisation using it.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The answer is
not to slow digital transformation. It is to make that transformation
resilient.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Cybersecurity
cannot be the security layer added after digital infrastructure has been built.
It must be part of the architecture from the beginning.<o:p></o:p></p><p>



















</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Otherwise,
Africa risks building highly connected maritime systems without adequately
understanding what happens when those connections fail.<o:p></o:p></p><h3>The Intelligence Gap</h3><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">This is where
the conversation must move beyond conventional cybersecurity.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Africa does not
only need stronger cyber defences.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It needs better
maritime cyber intelligence.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Knowing that a
system has been attacked is useful.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Knowing where
vulnerabilities are emerging before they are exploited is far more valuable.
But the intelligence challenge goes further.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">What happens
when a cyber vulnerability<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">intersects with
a physical maritime risk?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">What happens
when a technology dependency sits within a critical trade corridor?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">What happens
when a third-party system supporting a port is compromised?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">What happens
when a disruption lasts for hours rather than minutes?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And who is
monitoring the consequences across the wider maritime ecosystem?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">These questions
require information from multiple domains.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Vessel data.
Port intelligence.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Cyber-threat
information. Cargo flows.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Infrastructure
data.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Trade patterns.
Geopolitical developments. Operational performance.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Financial
exposure.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The objective
is not simply to know that an attack occurred.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It is to
understand what the attack could do to the maritime economy before the
consequences become visible.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Which maritime
systems are most exposed? Which vulnerabilities are repeatedly targeted?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Which
third-party dependencies create systemic risk?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Where are
digital and physical maritime risks<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">converging?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Which trade
corridors would be most vulnerable to prolonged digital disruption?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">What would
happen to cargo flows if a critical port system went offline for 24 hours?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Seventy-two
hours? A week?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And who would
coordinate the response?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">These are not
questions for cybersecurity specialists alone.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">They concern port
authorities, shipping companies, regulators, insurers, banks, logistics
operators, technology providers, national security institutions and economic
policymakers.<o:p></o:p></p><p>

























































</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Cyber
intelligence is becoming part of maritime intelligence.<o:p></o:p></p><h3>The Greatest Risk May Be Between Systems</h3><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">One of the most
underestimated vulnerabilities may not lie inside individual systems.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It may lie
between them.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Maritime trade
works through connections. Ships communicate with shore-based systems.
Terminals exchange information with shipping lines.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Ports interact
with customs.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Logistics
providers exchange cargo data. Banks support transactions.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Insurers
process risk information. Government agencies share regulatory and security
information.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">These
connections create efficiency. They also create systemic exposure.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A failure in
one part of the network can create consequences elsewhere.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">This means
cybersecurity maturity cannot be measured simply by asking whether an
organisation has firewalls, passwords, security policies or compliance certificates.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Those are
important.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">But they do not
answer the larger question: What happens when the system you depend on<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">the system you
do not control?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That is where
institutional blind spots can emerge.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">One
organisation may assume another is managing the risk.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The other may
assume the same thing. And somewhere between them, a critical vulnerability can
remain invisible until it becomes an operational problem.<o:p></o:p></p><p>





























</p><p>The weakest
point in a connected maritime chain may not be the weakest organisation. It may
be the least visible dependency.<br><span style="color: rgb(21, 28, 46); font-family: &quot;Playfair Display&quot;, &quot;Playfair Display Fallback&quot;, Georgia, serif; font-size: 18px; font-weight: 600;">Who owns the Risk</span></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">This may be one
of the most important questions Africa’s maritime sector needs to<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">When a cyber
incident affects a port, who owns the risk?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The port
authority? The terminal operator?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The technology
provider? The shipping line?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The national
cybersecurity authority? The regulator?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And if the
consequences spread across borders, who coordinates the response?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Maritime
operations already cross institutional and national boundaries.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Digital
dependencies make those boundaries even more complicated.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A port may
control its terminal.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A terminal may
control its operating system. A technology provider may control the underlying
platform.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A shipping
company may depend on another digital service.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Customs may
rely on a separate interface. The result is a chain of dependencies in which
operational responsibility and cyber responsibility do not always sit in the
same place.<o:p></o:p></p><p>























</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That is not
merely a technical problem. It is a governance problem.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%"><span style="color: rgb(21, 28, 46); font-family: &quot;Playfair Display&quot;, &quot;Playfair Display Fallback&quot;, Georgia, serif; font-size: 18px; font-weight: 600;">Cyber Resilience Is Becoming a Financial Issue</span></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">There is
another dimension Africa’s maritime sector cannot ignore:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Capital.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Investors care
about operational continuity.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Banks care
about the risks surrounding the assets and businesses they finance.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Insurers price
exposure.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Port operators
absorb the cost of downtime. Shipping companies carry the consequences of
operational disruption.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">As maritime
infrastructure becomes more digital, cyber resilience will increasingly
influence how these risks are assessed.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A highly
automated port with weak recovery capability may carry a different risk profile
from a comparable port with tested resilience.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A shipping
company dependent on poorly protected digital infrastructure may present
greater operational exposure.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A technology
platform supporting critical maritime functions may itself become a material
business risk if its availability cannot be assured.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Cyber
resilience is therefore moving closer to the language of: bankability.
insurability. investment.</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The issue is no
longer simply whether an organisation can afford cybersecurity.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">

























</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It is whether
investors, insurers and financiers can afford to ignore cyber resilience when
assessing maritime assets and businesses.<o:p></o:p></p><h3>The Human and Institutional</h3><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Technology is
only one part of the problem. People remain central to cybersecurity.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Employees,
contractors, vendors and third-party service providers interact with the
systems that keep maritime operations moving.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">But the deeper
issue is institutional.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Do boards
understand their cyber exposure? Do executives know which digital systems are
operationally critical?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Are
incident-response plans actually tested? Are recovery procedures practised?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Do
organisations know how long they can operate if a critical system becomes
unavailable? Do they know which functions can be performed manually?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Do they have
alternative communication channels?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And when a
disruption crosses institutional boundaries, does anyone have the authority to
coordinate the response?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Cyber
resilience is therefore not simply a technology problem.<o:p></o:p></p><p>

















</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It is a
governance, preparedness and leadership problem.<o:p></o:p></p><h3>Africa Needs Resilience, Not Illusion of Perfect Security</h3><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">No digital
system can realistically be guaranteed to be immune from disruption.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The strategic
objective should therefore not be an unrealistic promise of perfect security.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It should be
resilience.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Can critical
operations be maintained?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Can threats be
detected early?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Can compromised
systems be isolated? Can essential data be recovered?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Can operations
continue through alternative processes?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Can institutions
coordinate rapidly?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Can the system
learn from an incident and become stronger afterwards?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That requires:
Redundancy.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Incident-response
capabilities. Recovery plans.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Alternative
communication channels. Clear command structures.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Threat
intelligence.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Regular
testing.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And
institutional learning.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The objective
is not perfect security.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It is the
capacity to keep critical maritime functions operating when security fails.<o:p></o:p></p><p>

































</p><p>That is the
difference between cybersecurity and cyber resilience.<br></p><h3>The Regulatory Challenge<o:p></o:p></h3><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Cyber risk also

however, a strategic opportunity within this challenge.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Africa does not
have to approach maritime cybersecurity solely as a defensive exercise.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It can develop
a stronger African maritime cyber-intelligence capability that connects
information traditionally held in separate silos.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Imagine
bringing together:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">vessel data +
port intelligence + cyber threats + cargo flows + infrastructure data + geopolitical
developments + operational performance.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The objective
would be to identify relationships between digital and physical risks.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Where could a
cyber vulnerability disrupt a critical trade corridor?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Which
technology dependencies create systemic exposure?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Which ports or
maritime assets have the greatest concentration of digital risk?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Where could
cyber risk alter insurance, investment or financing assumptions?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Which emerging
threats deserve intervention before they become operational crises?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And perhaps
most importantly:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Who has the
intelligence to see the threat before everyone else sees the consequences?<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That is where
cybersecurity becomes more than an IT function.<o:p></o:p></p><p>



























</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It becomes
strategic maritime intelligence.<o:p></o:p></p><h3>The Cost Of Waiting</h3><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Cyber
resilience is often treated as an expenditure that can be postponed until an
incident occurs.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That
calculation is increasingly difficult to justify. The cost of prevention is
visible.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The cost of
disruption is not.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It can include
vessel delays, terminal congestion, cargo backlogs, failed transactions,
disrupted logistics, contractual penalties, reputational damage and lost trade.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And because
maritime systems are interconnected, the impact can extend well beyond the
organisation initially compromised.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Africa
therefore faces a strategic choice.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It can continue
digitising first and addressing cyber resilience later.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Or it can
recognise a more fundamental reality:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Digital
infrastructure is now part of critical maritime infrastructure.<o:p></o:p></p><p>

















</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And critical
infrastructure cannot be treated as secure simply because the physical asset
remains standing.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%"><br></p><h2>PRIMEAXIS INSIGHT</h2><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Africa’s
maritime cybersecurity debate should move beyond passwords, firewalls and
compliance.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The larger
issue is how Africa defines maritime security in an increasingly digital
economy.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">For too long,
security has often been strengthened after disruption.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Digitalisation
changes that logic.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The next major
maritime disruption may not arrive as a visible attack on a vessel, port or
terminal.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It may begin
quietly inside the systems that coordinate them.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It may exploit
a third-party dependency. It may cross institutional boundaries.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It may not
immediately look like a maritime security incident at all.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Yet its
consequences could eventually be measured in delayed vessels, congested
terminals, disrupted cargo flows, financial losses and interrupted trade.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That is why the
conversation must move from cybersecurity to cyber resilience, and from cyber
defence to cyber intelligence.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The next
generation of maritime competitiveness will not be determined only by who
builds the largest port, operates the biggest terminal or deploys the most
sophisticated technology.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It will
increasingly be determined by who can keep critical maritime systems
functioning under pressure.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">That makes
cyber resilience: an economic issue.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">A trade issue.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">An investment
issue. A governance issue.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">And ultimately:<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">a maritime
intelligence issue.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">Africa’s
maritime future will be digital.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">The strategic
question is no longer whether Africa will digitise.<o:p></o:p></p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">





































</p><p class="MsoNormal" style="margin-bottom:7.0pt;line-height:107%">It is whether
Africa can build a digital maritime economy resilient enough to keep trade
moving when the invisible disruption comes.<o:p></o:p></p>
