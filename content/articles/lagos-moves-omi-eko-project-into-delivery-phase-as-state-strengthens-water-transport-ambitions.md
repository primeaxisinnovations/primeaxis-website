<p>The Lagos State Government has moved the Omi Eko Project into a critical execution phase with the commencement of the Project Management, Operations and Design (PMOD) Consultancy, signalling a transition from infrastructure planning to project delivery.</p><p>Beyond marking another implementation milestone, the development reflects Lagos' broader strategy to position water transportation as a central pillar of its urban mobility system amid rapid population growth, worsening road congestion and the increasing demand for sustainable transport solutions.</p><p>Speaking at the PMOD Consultancy Kick-off Meeting in Lagos, the Commissioner for Transportation, Oluwaseun Osiyemi, described the consultancy as a strategic partnership that will provide the technical leadership, operational planning and project management expertise required to deliver the flagship initiative.</p><p>"The Omi Eko Project represents one of the most ambitious investments in inland waterways transportation in Africa," Osiyemi said.</p><p>According to the Commissioner, the project extends beyond the construction of terminals and the deployment of electric ferries. It is designed to improve connectivity across the state, reduce travel times, enhance passenger safety, stimulate economic activity and advance environmental sustainability through cleaner transport solutions.</p><p><img src="https://n0jyvquhgs2welj7.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/uploads/1784121309409-015b799a-whatsapp-image-2026-07-15-at-14-05-04.webp" alt=""><span style="color: rgb(21, 28, 46); font-family: &quot;Playfair Display&quot;, &quot;Playfair Display Fallback&quot;, Georgia, serif; font-size: 18px; font-weight: 600;">Why the PMOD Consultancy Matters</span></p><p>Large transport infrastructure projects are often judged by the assets they deliver. Their long-term success, however, depends just as much on how effectively they are planned, coordinated and operated.</p><p>The appointment of a Project Management, Operations and Design consultant introduces an institutional layer expected to oversee project execution, coordinate stakeholders and ensure that operational readiness develops alongside physical infrastructure.</p><p>For a project of Omi Eko's scale, integrating design, operations and implementation from the outset could reduce delivery risks while improving long-term operational sustainability.</p><p>Osiyemi noted that the consultant's responsibilities will extend beyond project management to include stakeholder coordination and implementation support throughout the delivery process.</p><h3>International Development Partners Deepen Their Support</h3><p>The project also underscores the growing role of international development finance in advancing urban transport transformation across Africa.</p><p>The Commissioner acknowledged the continued partnership of the French Development Agency (AFD), the European Investment Bank (EIB) and the European Union (EU), describing their support as a vote of confidence in Lagos State's long-term mobility strategy.</p><p>Beyond financing, such partnerships increasingly provide technical expertise, governance frameworks and international best practices that strengthen project delivery.</p><p>Representing the PMOD Consultancy, Dimitris Galanis, Head of Delegation and Director of the International Unit at Planet SA, said the consultancy would apply international best practices in project management, operational planning and technical delivery throughout the implementation process.</p><p><img src="https://n0jyvquhgs2welj7.public.blob.vercel-storage.com/uploads/1784121325022-9d34e678-whatsapp-image-2026-07-15-at-14-05-05.webp" alt=""><br></p><h3>Waterways Gain Strategic Importance</h3><p>As Lagos continues to expand, pressure on its road transport network continues to intensify.</p><p>With its extensive coastline and inland waterways, the state has increasingly identified water transportation as a strategic alternative capable of supporting a more integrated, resilient and multimodal transport network.</p><p>The Omi Eko Project seeks to unlock that potential through investments in modern terminals, electric ferry services, improved operational systems and an enhanced passenger experience.</p><p>If successfully implemented, the project could strengthen connectivity across Lagos while contributing to broader environmental and economic objectives.</p><p><br></p><h3>What This Means</h3><p>The commencement of the PMOD consultancy represents more than another procurement milestone. It signals a growing recognition that delivering complex transport infrastructure requires more than capital investment.</p><p>Across many emerging economies, infrastructure projects have struggled not because of inadequate funding, but because operational planning, governance and stakeholder coordination received insufficient attention during implementation.</p><p>By embedding these functions within the project's delivery framework, Lagos is seeking to reduce execution risks while improving the long-term viability and performance of its investment.</p><p><br></p><h3>&nbsp;PrimeAxis Insight</h3><p>The future of urban mobility in Africa will not be determined solely by how much infrastructure governments build, but by how effectively that infrastructure is planned, managed and integrated into broader transport systems.</p><p>The Omi Eko Project reflects an important shift in thinking—from viewing water transportation as an alternative mode of travel to recognising it as strategic urban infrastructure capable of supporting economic productivity, environmental sustainability and metropolitan resilience.</p><p>The decision to commence the PMOD consultancy is therefore significant. It acknowledges that successful infrastructure delivery requires more than engineering excellence; it demands institutional coordination, operational readiness and disciplined project governance.</p><p>For Lagos, success will ultimately be measured not simply by the completion of terminals or the deployment of electric ferries, but by its ability to build a reliable, commercially sustainable and passenger-focused water transport system that reshapes mobility across one of Africa's fastest-growing cities.</p>
