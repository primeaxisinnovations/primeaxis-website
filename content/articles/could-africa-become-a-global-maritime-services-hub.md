<p>When people think about maritime, they usually think about ships.</p><p>Others think about ports.</p><p>Some think about containers.</p><p>Very few think about the services that make global shipping possible.</p><p><br></p><p>Yet this is where some of the greatest value in the maritime economy is created.</p><p>A vessel may sail through African waters.</p><p>Its cargo may be destined for African markets.</p><p>Its crew may call at African ports.</p><p>But the insurance may be arranged in London.</p><p>The financing may come from Singapore.</p><p>The shipbroker may be in Athens.</p><p>The legal dispute may be settled in Dubai.</p><p>The vessel may be classified in Europe.</p><p>The digital platform managing its voyage may be headquartered elsewhere.</p><p>The cargo passes through Africa.</p><p>The value does not.</p><p>This is one of the least discussed gaps in Africa's Blue Economy.</p><p><br></p><p>For decades, Africa has focused on facilitating trade.</p><p>The next frontier is enabling trade.</p><p>That means building industries around maritime services—not simply infrastructure.</p><p>A globally competitive maritime ecosystem is built on more than ports. It is built on institutions, expertise, finance, technology, regulation, and professional services.</p><p>Imagine an Africa where:</p><p>● Marine insurance is underwritten by African firms.</p><p>● Shipbrokers negotiate regional and international chartering deals.</p><p>● Maritime financial institutions finance African fleets.</p><p>●Arbitration centres resolve commercial disputes on the continent.</p><p>●Classification societies certify vessels to global standards.</p><p>●African technology companies develop digital solutions for ports, shipping, customs, and logistics.</p><p>These industries create high-value jobs.</p><p>They generate exportable expertise.</p><p>They attract investment.</p><p>Most importantly, they enable Africa to capture value long after a ship leaves the harbour.</p><p>The opportunity is significant.</p><p>Africa sits astride some of the world's busiest maritime trade routes and depends on shipping for the overwhelming majority of its international trade. The continent is also home to a young and growing workforce, expanding financial markets, and a rapidly evolving digital economy.</p><p><br></p><p>The ingredients already exist.</p><p>What remains is connecting them through deliberate policy, regional cooperation, investment in skills, and stronger institutions.</p><p>The future of African maritime competitiveness will not be determined solely by the number of ports we build.</p><p>It will be determined by the number of globally competitive maritime businesses we build around those ports.</p><p><br></p><p>●Infrastructure enables trade.</p><p>●Services multiply value.</p><p>●Ownership creates prosperity.</p><p><br></p><p>The countries that dominate global maritime do all three.</p><p>Africa can too.</p><p>The question is no longer whether Africa should participate in global shipping.</p><p>The question is whether Africa is ready to own more of the business that surrounds it.</p><p>Because the greatest opportunity may not lie in moving more cargo.</p><p>It may lie in owning more of the maritime economy.</p><p><br></p><p>That is how Africa moves from being a participant in global trade to becoming a global maritime powerhouse.</p>
