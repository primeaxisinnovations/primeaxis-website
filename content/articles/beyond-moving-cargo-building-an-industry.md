<h2 style="margin: 20px 0px 10px; font-weight: 600; font-family: &quot;Playfair Display&quot;, &quot;Playfair Display Fallback&quot;, Georgia, serif; --tw-font-weight: 600; color: rgb(21, 28, 46);">Beyond Moving Cargo. Building an Industry.</h2><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">When people think about maritime, they usually think about ships.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Others think about ports.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Some think about containers.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Very few think about the services that make global shipping possible.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Yet this is where some of the greatest value in the maritime economy is created.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">A vessel may sail through African waters.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Its cargo may be destined for African markets.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Its crew may call at African ports.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">But the insurance may be arranged in London.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The financing may come from Singapore.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The shipbroker may be in Athens.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The legal dispute may be settled in Dubai.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The vessel may be classified in Europe.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The digital platform managing its voyage may be headquartered elsewhere.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The cargo passes through Africa.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The value does not.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">This is one of the least discussed gaps in Africa's Blue Economy.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;"><br></p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">For decades, Africa has focused on facilitating trade.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The next frontier is enabling trade.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">That means building industries around maritime services—not simply infrastructure.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">A globally competitive maritime ecosystem is built on more than ports. It is built on institutions, expertise, finance, technology, regulation, and professional services.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Imagine an Africa where:</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;"></p><ul style="margin: 0px 0px 12px; padding: 0px 0px 0px 24px;"><li>Marine insurance is underwritten by African firms.</li><li>Shipbrokers negotiate regional and international chartering deals.</li><li>Maritime financial institutions finance African fleets.</li><li>Arbitration centres resolve commercial disputes on the continent.</li><li>Classification societies certify vessels to global standards.</li><li>African technology companies develop digital solutions for ports, shipping, customs, and logistics.</li></ul><p></p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;"><br></p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">These industries create high-value jobs.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">They generate exportable expertise.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">They attract investment.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Most importantly, they enable Africa to capture value long after a ship leaves the harbour.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The opportunity is significant.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Africa sits astride some of the world's busiest maritime trade routes and depends on shipping for the overwhelming majority of its international trade. The continent is also home to a young and growing workforce, expanding financial markets, and a rapidly evolving digital economy.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;"><br></p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The ingredients already exist.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">What remains is connecting them through deliberate policy, regional cooperation, investment in skills, and stronger institutions.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The future of African maritime competitiveness will not be determined solely by the number of ports we build.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">It will be determined by the number of globally competitive maritime businesses we build around those ports.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;"></p><ul style="margin: 0px 0px 12px; padding: 0px 0px 0px 24px;"><li>Infrastructure enables trade.</li><li>Services multiply value.</li><li>Ownership creates prosperity.</li></ul><p></p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;"><br></p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The countries that dominate global maritime do all three.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Africa can too.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The question is no longer whether Africa should participate in global shipping.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">The question is whether Africa is ready to own more of the business that surrounds it.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">Because the greatest opportunity may not lie in moving more cargo.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;">It may lie in owning more of the maritime economy.</p><p style="margin: 0px 0px 12px;"><span style="font-weight: bolder;"><i>That is how Africa moves from being a participant in global trade to becoming a global maritime powerhouse.</i></span></p>
