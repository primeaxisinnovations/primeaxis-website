<p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;" id="docs-internal-guid-5ef4b00a-7fff-5af7-9a8b-5eb81b50a721"><span style="background-color: transparent; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; font-size: 11pt; white-space: pre-wrap;">Nigeria has spent years trying to make trade administration less fragmented. The National Single Window, which went live on March 27, 2026, is the most ambitious attempt yet to bring government agencies, documentation, payments and trade processes into a connected digital environment.</span></p><br><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">The numbers show adoption. By September, the platform had processed more than 124,614 licences and permits, registered 11,096 importers and agents and facilitated about ₦12.59 billion in regulatory payments. The Federal Government has set the end of November as the deadline for Phase Two, which is expected to deepen the export side of the reform.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;"></span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">But adoption is not the same as transformation.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">The harder question is whether the Single Window will eventually reduce the time, cost and uncertainty attached to moving goods through Nigeria.</span></p><br><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">That distinction matters because the cost of trade is rarely created by one transaction. It accumulates across permits, regulatory approvals, documentation, cargo examination, terminal processes, trucking, road access, storage, demurrage, inland transportation and, ultimately, the journey to the customer.</span></p><br><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">A faster screen cannot compensate for a slower supply chain.</span></p><h2><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">BEYOND THE SCREEN</span></h2><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">Nigeria's non oil export story makes the question more urgent.</span></p><br><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">The country recorded US$6.1 billion in non oil export receipts in 2025, with export volumes reaching 8.02 million metric tonnes. The Nigerian Export Promotion Council says seaports handled 94 percent of those exports across 20 exit points.</span></p><br><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">That means the competitiveness of Nigerian exports increasingly depends not only on what happens inside government portals, but on what happens after a document is approved.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">This is where the distinction between digital reform and trade reform becomes important.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">Abdullazeez Babatunde Mukaila, a freight forwarder and former National Secretary of the Association of Nigerian Licensed Customs Agents, sees the National Single Window as necessary but incomplete if the underlying regulatory structure remains fragmented.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">From his experience, multiple agencies can still exercise overlapping authority over the same categories of goods. He said industry consultations had identified more than 40 products affected by conflicting regulatory mandates, pointing to the need for clearer institutional boundaries and stronger coordination.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">The problem, therefore, is not simply whether agencies are connected to a platform.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">It is whether the platform is connecting agencies that have already agreed on who does what.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">Digitalising duplication is not the same as eliminating duplication.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">The National Single Window itself encountered some of these realities during its early implementation. In May, its management acknowledged 9,800 operational cases involving licences, permits and certificates, with data migration difficulties, documentation gaps and overlapping regulatory mandates identified among the causes. It said 99 percent of the cases had subsequently been resolved.</span></p><br><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">That experience offers an important lesson.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">&nbsp;Technology can expose institutional friction. It cannot independently resolve the policy dispute behind it.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">WHEN DELAY BECOMES A TRADE COST</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">For businesses, the economic consequence of fragmented processes is not theoretical.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">Every additional day can translate into storage charges, demurrage, trucking costs, inventory financing and uncertainty over delivery schedules.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">Mukaila argues that greater transparency and predictability are therefore as important as digitisation itself. Importers and exporters need to know what is payable, who is responsible for each stage of the process, what standard applies and how disputes will be resolved.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">This is where the Single Window must eventually move from being a platform for transactions to becoming an instrument for predictability.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; Nigerian ports handled 35.7 million metric tonnes of cargo in the second quarter of 2026, up 12.3 percent from the same period in 2025. Outward cargo increased by 22 percent, while container traffic rose to 602,392 TEUs.</span></p><br><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">But higher throughput does not automatically mean lower trade costs.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">The NPA itself has had to intervene repeatedly in operational problems along the port corridors. In June, the Authority convened stakeholders over persistent congestion around Apapa and Tin Can, with illegal extortion points, overlapping security responsibilities and other operational distortions identified as contributors to delays. A multi agency task force was subsequently established to monitor and address disruptions.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">By August, the NPA said the task force had dismantled more than 1,500 illegally parked trucks and unauthorised checkpoints along the Apapa corridors.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">These interventions are important. They also reveal the gap between the ambition of a digitally enabled trade system and the physical realities confronting cargo.</span></p><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt; font-family: Calibri, sans-serif; color: rgb(0, 0, 0); background-color: transparent; font-variant: normal; vertical-align: baseline; white-space: pre-wrap;">The port authority cannot solve every problem in the trade chain. But where cargo movement is delayed by congestion, access restrictions, weak coordination or avoidable operational friction, the economic cost eventually sits with the trader.</span></p><br><p dir="ltr" style="line-height:1.3800000000000001;margin-top:0.0pt;margin-bottom:10.0pt;"><span style="font-size: 11pt;
